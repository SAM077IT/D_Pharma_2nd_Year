# Chapter 12 — Ophthalmology, Antimicrobial Resistance & Women's Health
**Conjunctivitis, Glaucoma, AMR, PCOS, Dysmenorrhoea, PMS**

## A. Conjunctivitis

### Bacterial
Red eye with purulent discharge may occur.

### Viral
Often watery discharge and may be associated with respiratory infection.

### Management
- Hygiene and handwashing
- Avoid sharing towels/cosmetics
- Antibiotics are not useful for uncomplicated viral conjunctivitis
- Appropriate treatment for bacterial disease depends on clinical assessment

## B. Glaucoma
A group of optic neuropathies associated with characteristic optic nerve damage; intraocular pressure is an important risk factor.

### Drug classes
- Prostaglandin analogues
- Beta-blockers
- Alpha-2 agonists
- Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors
- Miotics in selected situations

### Counselling
Correct eye-drop technique and adherence are critical.

## C. Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)

### Definition
Resistance occurs when microorganisms no longer respond to medicines that previously worked against them.

### Causes
- Unnecessary antimicrobial use
- Incorrect drug/dose/duration
- Poor adherence
- Self-medication
- Inadequate infection prevention
- Agricultural/healthcare misuse

### Pharmacist role
- Encourage appropriate use
- Counsel on adherence
- Avoid unnecessary antibiotics
- Promote infection prevention
- Support antimicrobial stewardship

## D. Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)
A common endocrine/metabolic disorder associated with ovulatory dysfunction and/or hyperandrogenism after appropriate diagnostic assessment.

### Features
Irregular periods, acne/hirsutism, infertility and metabolic abnormalities may occur.

### Management
- Lifestyle/weight management where appropriate
- Combined hormonal contraceptives for cycle/hyperandrogenism management in suitable patients
- Metformin in selected patients, particularly with metabolic indications
- Fertility-directed treatment when pregnancy is desired

## E. Dysmenorrhoea
Painful menstruation.

### Management
- NSAIDs are commonly first-line for primary dysmenorrhoea
- Heat and exercise may help
- Hormonal therapy in selected patients
- Evaluate secondary causes if symptoms are atypical/severe

## F. Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS)
Cyclical physical and emotional symptoms occurring before menstruation and improving after menstruation begins.

### Management
Lifestyle measures, symptom tracking, NSAIDs for pain, and selected hormonal/psychological/pharmacological options depending on severity.
