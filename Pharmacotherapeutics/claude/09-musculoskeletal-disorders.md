# Pharmacotherapeutics — Chapter 2(h)
## Musculoskeletal Disorders: Rheumatoid Arthritis · Osteoarthritis

> **Weightage:** 3 hrs · **3-day plan:** Day 2 — last topic of the day

---

## 1. Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)

**Definition:** Chronic systemic **autoimmune** inflammatory disease primarily affecting synovial joints, symmetric in distribution.

**Etiopathogenesis:** Autoimmune process (genetic + environmental triggers) → synovial inflammation (pannus formation) → cartilage/bone destruction; associated with **Rheumatoid Factor (RF)** and **Anti-CCP antibodies**.

**Clinical manifestations:** Symmetric polyarthritis (small joints — hands, wrists, feet), **morning stiffness lasting >1 hour**, joint swelling/warmth, fatigue, low-grade fever; extra-articular — rheumatoid nodules, interstitial lung disease.

**Non-pharmacological management:** Physiotherapy, occupational therapy (joint protection techniques), regular exercise, rest during flares, weight management, smoking cessation.

**Pharmacological management:**
| Class | Examples | Role |
|---|---|---|
| DMARDs (conventional) | **Methotrexate** | 1st-line, disease-modifying — slows joint destruction |
| DMARDs (biologic) | Etanercept, Adalimumab (anti-TNF) | Moderate-severe/refractory disease |
| NSAIDs | Ibuprofen, Naproxen | Symptomatic pain/inflammation relief (not disease-modifying) |
| Corticosteroids | Prednisolone | Bridge therapy during flares, short-term |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Why is Methotrexate the anchor drug in RA, and what must be co-prescribed?</summary>
It is the first-line DMARD — effective, well-studied, and slows radiographic progression. Folic acid supplementation is co-prescribed to reduce Methotrexate's mucosal/GI toxicity and hepatotoxicity.
</details>

## 2. Osteoarthritis (OA)

**Definition:** Degenerative joint disease from progressive **cartilage breakdown**, a "wear and tear" disorder — the most common form of arthritis, typically asymmetric.

**Etiopathogenesis:** Mechanical stress, ageing, obesity, joint trauma/overuse → cartilage degeneration → subchondral bone changes, osteophyte formation. **Not autoimmune** (key contrast with RA).

**Clinical manifestations:** Joint pain **worse with activity, relieved by rest**, **morning stiffness <30 minutes** (brief — key contrast with RA), commonly affects weight-bearing joints (knee, hip) and DIP/PIP joints of hands (Heberden's/Bouchard's nodes), crepitus.

**Key differentiator (exam favorite — RA vs OA):**
| Feature | Rheumatoid Arthritis | Osteoarthritis |
|---|---|---|
| Pathology | Autoimmune, inflammatory | Degenerative, mechanical |
| Joint distribution | Symmetric, small joints | Asymmetric, weight-bearing joints |
| Morning stiffness | >1 hour | <30 minutes |
| Systemic features | Yes (fatigue, fever, nodules) | No |
| Key markers | RF, Anti-CCP positive | No serological markers |

**Non-pharmacological management:** Weight reduction (reduces joint load), low-impact exercise, physiotherapy, assistive devices (cane, braces), joint protection.

**Pharmacological management:**
| Class | Examples | Role |
|---|---|---|
| First-line analgesic | Paracetamol | Mild disease |
| NSAIDs (oral/topical) | Ibuprofen, Diclofenac gel | Moderate pain, when paracetamol insufficient |
| Intra-articular injections | Corticosteroids, Hyaluronic acid | Localized severe pain, refractory to oral therapy |
| Surgery | Joint replacement (arthroplasty) | End-stage disease |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Why isn't Methotrexate/DMARD therapy used in OA?</summary>
OA is a mechanical/degenerative process, not autoimmune-inflammatory — there is no immune-mediated joint destruction for a DMARD to modify. Management focuses on symptom control and mechanical load reduction.
</details>

## Quick Revision Table

| Feature | RA | OA |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Autoimmune | Degenerative |
| Distribution | Symmetric, small joints | Asymmetric, weight-bearing |
| Morning stiffness | >1 hr | <30 min |
| 1st-line drug | Methotrexate (DMARD) | Paracetamol/NSAIDs |

## Self-Test

<details>
<summary>Q1. Differentiate RA and OA based on morning stiffness duration.</summary>
RA: morning stiffness lasts more than 1 hour. OA: morning stiffness lasts less than 30 minutes.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q2. What is the first-line DMARD for RA, and what vitamin should accompany it?</summary>
Methotrexate, co-prescribed with folic acid to reduce toxicity.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q3. Which serological markers support a diagnosis of RA?</summary>
Rheumatoid Factor (RF) and Anti-CCP antibodies.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q4. Name the classic hand joint nodules seen in osteoarthritis.</summary>
Heberden's nodes (DIP joints) and Bouchard's nodes (PIP joints).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q5. Why is NSAID therapy alone considered inadequate management for RA?</summary>
NSAIDs only control symptoms (pain/inflammation) but do not slow or prevent the underlying joint destruction — a DMARD is needed to modify disease progression.
</details>
