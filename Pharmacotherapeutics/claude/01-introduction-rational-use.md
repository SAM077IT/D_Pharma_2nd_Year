## 1. Pharmacotherapeutics — Introduction, Scope & Objectives

**Definition:** Pharmacotherapeutics is the branch of pharmacy/medicine dealing with the *use of drugs in the treatment of disease* — selecting the right drug, dose, route, and duration for a given patient.

**Scope:**
- Etiopathogenesis of common diseases
- Evidence-based selection of drug therapy
- Individualized therapeutic plans based on diagnosis, age, renal/hepatic function, comorbidities
- Monitoring clinical outcomes (efficacy + safety) of drug therapy

**Objectives (course outcomes you'll be tested on):**
1. Assess subjective (symptoms) and objective (signs, labs) parameters of patients
2. Assist in identifying and resolving **drug-related problems (DRPs)**
3. Participate in rational medicine therapy planning
4. Design/deliver **discharge counselling**

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: What is a Drug-Related Problem (DRP)?</summary>

An event/circumstance involving drug therapy that actually or potentially interferes with a desired health outcome. Common categories: untreated indication, improper drug selection, sub-therapeutic dose, overdose, adverse drug reaction, drug interaction, failure to receive drug (non-adherence), drug use without indication.
</details>

## 2. Rational Use of Medicines (RUM)

**WHO Definition:** *"Patients receive medications appropriate to their clinical needs, in doses that meet their own individual requirements, for an adequate period of time, and at the lowest cost to them and their community."*

### 4 Pillars of Rational Use (easy to remember — "Right x 4")
| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Right drug | Correct choice for the diagnosis |
| Right dose | Appropriate for age/weight/renal-hepatic status |
| Right duration | Neither too short (relapse/resistance) nor too long (toxicity) |
| Right cost | Most affordable effective option |

**Causes of Irrational Use:** polypharmacy, overuse of antibiotics/injections, self-medication, non-adherence to STGs, failure to prescribe generics.

**WHO's core interventions to promote RUM:**
- Establishing a multidisciplinary national body to monitor medicine use
- Use of clinical guidelines (STGs)
- Essential Medicines List based on treatment of choice
- Drug and Therapeutics Committees (DTCs) in hospitals
- Problem-based pharmacotherapy training in undergraduate curricula
- Continuing in-service medical education
- Supervision, audit, and feedback
- Independent (unbiased) drug information
- Public education about medicines
- Avoiding perverse financial incentives
- Enforcing appropriate regulation
- Sufficient government expenditure to ensure availability of medicines/staff

## 3. Evidence-Based Medicine (EBM)

**Definition (Sackett):** *"The conscientious, explicit, and judicious use of current best evidence in making decisions about the care of individual patients."*

**3 pillars of EBM (exam favorite):**
1. Best available research evidence
2. Clinical expertise
3. Patient values & preferences

### Hierarchy of Evidence (strongest → weakest) — draw this as a pyramid in your answer sheet
1. Systematic reviews & meta-analyses
2. Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs)
3. Cohort studies
4. Case-control studies
5. Case series/case reports
6. Expert opinion/editorials

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Why RCTs rank above cohort/case-control studies?</summary>

Randomization minimizes selection bias and confounding, allowing a more confident cause-effect conclusion between intervention and outcome — the closest study design to proving causality outside of a meta-analysis of multiple RCTs.
</details>

## 4. Essential Medicines List (EML)

**WHO Definition:** Medicines that satisfy the priority healthcare needs of a population, selected with due regard to disease prevalence, efficacy, safety, and comparative cost-effectiveness.

**Selection criteria for EML:**
- Adequate data on efficacy and safety from clinical studies
- Evidence of performance in a variety of settings
- Favourable benefit-to-harm ratio
- Comparative cost-effectiveness
- Preference for single, well-studied active compounds over combination products (unless the combination has a proven therapeutic advantage)

**Key facts:**
- First WHO Model List of Essential Medicines published in **1977**
- Revised every **2 years** by WHO Expert Committee
- India has its own **National List of Essential Medicines (NLEM)** — latest major revision **NLEM 2022**

## 5. Standard Treatment Guidelines (STGs)

**Definition:** Systematically developed statements to assist practitioner and patient decisions about appropriate healthcare for *specific clinical circumstances* — i.e., what to use for a given diagnosis, at what dose, for how long.

**Purpose/advantages:**
- Standardizes quality of care across prescribers
- Reduces irrational prescribing & polypharmacy
- Basis for drug procurement/EML preparation
- Useful for training and supervision of health workers
- Cost containment for patients & health system

**STG development process (brief):** situation analysis → selection of priority diseases → evidence review → drafting by expert panel → peer review/field testing → dissemination/Broadcasting → periodic revision.
