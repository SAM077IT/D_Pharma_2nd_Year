# Pharmacotherapeutics — Chapter 2(l)
## Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)

> **Weightage:** 2 hrs · **3-day plan:** Day 3

---

## 1. Definition

**Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR):** The ability of microorganisms (bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites) to survive and multiply despite exposure to drugs that would normally kill them or inhibit their growth — a natural evolutionary process, **accelerated by misuse and overuse of antimicrobials** in humans, animals, and agriculture.

---

## 2. Mechanisms of Antibiotic Resistance (high-yield, classify with examples)

| Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|
| Enzymatic drug inactivation | β-lactamase enzymes destroying penicillins/cephalosporins |
| Altered target site | Altered penicillin-binding proteins (MRSA) |
| Reduced drug uptake | Decreased outer membrane permeability in Gram-negatives |
| Efflux pumps | Active pumping of drug out of the bacterial cell |
| Bypass pathways | Alternative metabolic pathway avoiding the drug's target |

**Genetic basis:** Chromosomal mutation (vertical transfer) or acquisition of resistance genes via plasmids/transposons (horizontal transfer — conjugation, transformation, transduction) between bacteria, even across species.

---

## 3. Causes/Contributing Factors to AMR

- Overuse/misuse of antibiotics (inappropriate prescribing, self-medication, incomplete courses)
- Use of antibiotics in livestock/agriculture for growth promotion
- Poor infection prevention and control practices in healthcare settings
- Lack of access to quality diagnostics (empirical broad-spectrum use without culture/sensitivity)
- Poor sanitation and hygiene facilitating spread of resistant organisms
- Limited new antibiotic development (declining pharmaceutical pipeline)

---

## 4. Clinically Important Resistant Organisms (exam favorite — "superbugs")

| Organism | Resistance |
|---|---|
| MRSA | Methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus* |
| VRE | Vancomycin-resistant *Enterococcus* |
| ESBL-producing organisms | Extended-spectrum beta-lactamase — resistant to most penicillins/cephalosporins |
| CRE | Carbapenem-resistant *Enterobacteriaceae* |
| MDR-TB / XDR-TB | Multi-drug-resistant / Extensively drug-resistant Tuberculosis |

---

## 5. Prevention and Control Strategies (very high-yield)

**Antimicrobial Stewardship Programs (ASPs):** Coordinated interventions to promote appropriate use of antimicrobials — right drug, dose, duration; based on culture and sensitivity reports where possible.

**Key measures:**
- Rational prescribing — avoid unnecessary/broad-spectrum antibiotics when narrow-spectrum suffices
- Complete the full prescribed course (patient counselling point)
- Infection prevention and control (hand hygiene, sterilization, isolation of infected patients)
- Surveillance of resistance patterns (local antibiograms)
- Restricting over-the-counter antibiotic sales
- Regulating antibiotic use in agriculture/veterinary practice
- Public awareness campaigns
- Encouraging research/development of new antimicrobials and rapid diagnostics
- Vaccination (reduces infections, thus reducing antibiotic need)

**WHO's Global Action Plan on AMR (2015) — 5 strategic objectives:**
1. Improve awareness and understanding of AMR
2. Strengthen knowledge through surveillance and research
3. Reduce the incidence of infection through effective sanitation, hygiene, and infection prevention
4. Optimize the use of antimicrobial agents
5. Develop the economic case for sustainable investment in new medicines, diagnostics, vaccines

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: What is the role of a pharmacist in antimicrobial stewardship?</summary>
Reviewing prescriptions for appropriateness, counselling patients on completing the full course, discouraging self-medication/OTC antibiotic sales, promoting de-escalation from broad to narrow-spectrum therapy once culture results are available, and monitoring for adverse effects/interactions.
</details>

## Self-Test

<details>
<summary>Q1. Name the four main mechanisms by which bacteria develop antibiotic resistance.</summary>
Enzymatic drug inactivation, altered target site, reduced drug uptake/permeability, and efflux pumps (also bypass pathways).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q2. What does MRSA stand for and what does it signify?</summary>
Methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus* — resistant to beta-lactam antibiotics due to altered penicillin-binding protein.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q3. What is an Antimicrobial Stewardship Program?</summary>
A coordinated set of interventions to ensure appropriate antimicrobial prescribing (right drug, dose, duration) to optimize outcomes and limit resistance development.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q4. List any three contributing causes of AMR.</summary>
Any three of: antibiotic overuse/misuse, incomplete treatment courses, use in livestock/agriculture, poor infection control, lack of diagnostics leading to empirical broad-spectrum use, poor sanitation.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q5. Name the year WHO launched its Global Action Plan on AMR.</summary>
2015.
</details>
