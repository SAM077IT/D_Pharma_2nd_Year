# Pharmacotherapeutics — Chapter 2(f)
## Haematological Disorders: Iron Deficiency Anaemia · Megaloblastic Anaemia

> **Weightage:** 4 hrs · **3-day plan:** Day 2

---

## 1. Iron Deficiency Anaemia (IDA)

**Definition:** Anaemia due to insufficient iron for adequate haemoglobin synthesis — the most common anaemia worldwide.

**Etiopathogenesis:** Chronic blood loss (GI bleeding, menorrhagia — most common cause in adults), inadequate dietary intake, malabsorption (coeliac disease), increased demand (pregnancy, growth).

**Clinical manifestations:** Fatigue, pallor, dyspnoea on exertion, brittle nails, **koilonychia** (spoon-shaped nails), **pica** (craving non-food items e.g. ice/clay), glossitis, angular stomatitis.

**Labs:** ↓ Hb, **microcytic hypochromic** (↓MCV, ↓MCH), ↓ serum ferritin, ↓ serum iron, ↑ TIBC (total iron-binding capacity).

**Non-pharmacological management:** Iron-rich diet (red meat, leafy greens, legumes), vitamin C with meals (↑ iron absorption), identify and treat underlying cause of blood loss.

**Pharmacological management:**
| Route | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Oral | Ferrous sulfate, Ferrous fumarate, Ferrous gluconate | 1st line; take on empty stomach for best absorption (or with food if GI upset); continue 3–6 months after Hb normalizes to replenish stores |
| Parenteral | Iron sucrose, Ferric carboxymaltose | Used when oral not tolerated/malabsorption/severe deficiency |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Common oral iron adverse effects & counselling points</summary>
Constipation, nausea, black/dark stools (harmless, expected), metallic taste. Avoid taking with tea/coffee/antacids/calcium (↓ absorption); vitamin C enhances absorption.
</details>

## 2. Megaloblastic Anaemia

**Definition:** Anaemia characterized by impaired DNA synthesis leading to large, immature red blood cell precursors (megaloblasts) — caused by **Vitamin B12** and/or **Folate** deficiency.

**Etiopathogenesis:**
| Cause | Vitamin B12 deficiency | Folate deficiency |
|---|---|---|
| Common cause | Pernicious anaemia (autoimmune — loss of intrinsic factor), strict vegan diet, malabsorption (ileal disease), metformin use | Poor diet, alcoholism, pregnancy (↑ demand), certain drugs (Methotrexate, Phenytoin) |

**Clinical manifestations:** Fatigue, pallor, glossitis (beefy red tongue), **neurological symptoms specific to B12 deficiency** — peripheral neuropathy, subacute combined degeneration of spinal cord (paraesthesia, ataxia); folate deficiency does NOT cause neurological symptoms.

**Labs:** **Macrocytic** anaemia (↑MCV), hypersegmented neutrophils, ↓ Vitamin B12 and/or ↓ folate levels.

**Non-pharmacological management:** Dietary counselling (B12 — animal products; folate — leafy greens, legumes), treat underlying malabsorption cause.

**Pharmacological management:**
| Deficiency | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Vitamin B12 deficiency | IM/SC Cyanocobalamin or Hydroxocobalamin injections (oral if diet-related only, not malabsorption) |
| Folate deficiency | Oral Folic acid supplementation |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Why must B12 deficiency be ruled out before giving folic acid alone?</summary>
Giving folic acid alone in an undiagnosed B12 deficiency corrects the anaemia (masking the haematological picture) but does NOT prevent — and may worsen/unmask — the neurological damage of B12 deficiency (subacute combined degeneration).
</details>

## Self-Test

<details>
<summary>Q1. What nail change is characteristic of iron deficiency anaemia?</summary>
Koilonychia (spoon-shaped nails).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q2. What is the most common cause of iron deficiency anaemia in adult women?</summary>
Menorrhagia (chronic menstrual blood loss).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q3. Which vitamin deficiency among the megaloblastic anaemias causes neurological symptoms, and what is the classic finding?</summary>
Vitamin B12 deficiency — subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord (peripheral neuropathy, ataxia).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q4. Why should you avoid tea/coffee with oral iron supplements?</summary>
Tannins in tea/coffee bind iron and reduce its absorption.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q5. What is the danger of treating megaloblastic anaemia with folic acid alone without excluding B12 deficiency?</summary>
It corrects the anaemia but does not prevent the neurological damage of B12 deficiency, which can progress unrecognized.
</details>
