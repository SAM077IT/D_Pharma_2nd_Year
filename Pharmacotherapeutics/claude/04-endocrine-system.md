## 1. Diabetes Mellitus (DM)

**Definition:** Chronic metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycaemia due to defective insulin secretion, insulin action, or both.

**Diagnostic criteria (any one):** Fasting plasma glucose ≥126 mg/dL · 2-hr OGTT ≥200 mg/dL · Random glucose ≥200 mg/dL with symptoms · HbA1c ≥6.5%

**Classification & Etiopathogenesis:**
| Type | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Type 1 | Autoimmune β-cell destruction → absolute insulin deficiency; usually young-onset |
| Type 2 | Insulin resistance + relative insulin deficiency; linked to obesity/lifestyle; majority of cases |
| Gestational | Develops during pregnancy due to placental hormones ↑ insulin resistance |

**Clinical manifestations:** Polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, unexplained weight loss (esp. T1DM), fatigue, blurred vision, recurrent infections, delayed wound healing. Long-term complications: retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy, macrovascular (CAD, stroke, PVD).

**Non-pharmacological management:** Medical nutrition therapy (carbohydrate counting, low glycaemic index), regular physical activity, weight reduction, self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG), diabetes education, foot care.

**Pharmacological management:**
| Class | Examples | Mechanism/Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Biguanide | **Metformin** | 1st line T2DM — ↓ hepatic glucose output; no hypoglycaemia/weight gain |
| Sulfonylureas | Glimepiride, Glibenclamide | ↑ insulin secretion; risk of hypoglycaemia |
| DPP-4 inhibitors | Sitagliptin | ↑ incretin effect; weight-neutral |
| SGLT2 inhibitors | Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin | Glucosuria; cardio/renal protective |
| GLP-1 agonists | Liraglutide, Semaglutide | Weight loss, injectable |
| Thiazolidinediones | Pioglitazone | ↓ insulin resistance; fluid retention risk |
| Insulin | Regular, NPH, Glargine, etc. | Mandatory in T1DM; used in T2DM when oral agents fail |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Why is Metformin first-line for T2DM?</summary>
Effective glycaemic control without causing hypoglycaemia or weight gain, cardiovascular safety, and low cost. Contraindicated in renal impairment (risk of lactic acidosis).
</details>

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Somogyi effect vs Dawn phenomenon</summary>
Somogyi effect = rebound morning hyperglycaemia following nocturnal hypoglycaemia (counter-regulatory hormone surge). Dawn phenomenon = early-morning hyperglycaemia from normal circadian ↑ in growth hormone/cortisol, without preceding hypoglycaemia.
</details>

## 2. Thyroid Disorders

### (a) Hypothyroidism

**Definition:** Deficiency of thyroid hormone (T3/T4) production.

**Etiopathogenesis:** Hashimoto's thyroiditis (autoimmune — most common), iodine deficiency, post-thyroidectomy/radioiodine, drugs (lithium, amiodarone), congenital.

**Clinical manifestations:** Fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, dry skin, bradycardia, myxoedema, hoarseness of voice, menstrual irregularities.

**Labs:** ↑ TSH, ↓ Free T4 (primary hypothyroidism).

**Management:** Levothyroxine (synthetic T4) — lifelong replacement, dose titrated to TSH; take on empty stomach, avoid concurrent calcium/iron (↓ absorption).

### (b) Hyperthyroidism

**Definition:** Excess circulating thyroid hormone.

**Etiopathogenesis:** Graves' disease (autoimmune, TSH-receptor stimulating antibodies — most common cause), toxic multinodular goitre, toxic adenoma, thyroiditis.

**Clinical manifestations:** Weight loss despite increased appetite, heat intolerance, palpitations/tachycardia, tremors, sweating, anxiety, exophthalmos (Graves'-specific), goitre.

**Labs:** ↓ TSH, ↑ Free T4/T3.

**Management:**
| Class | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Thionamides | **Carbimazole/Methimazole**, Propylthiouracil (PTU) | Inhibit thyroid hormone synthesis; PTU preferred in 1st trimester pregnancy |
| Beta-blockers | Propranolol | Rapid symptom control (tremor, tachycardia) |
| Radioactive iodine (I-131) | — | Definitive ablative therapy |
| Surgery | Thyroidectomy | Large goitre/malignancy suspicion |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Key adverse effect to counsel on with Carbimazole/PTU</summary>
Agranulocytosis — advise patient to report sore throat/fever immediately; requires stopping the drug and urgent CBC.
</details>

## Quick Revision Table

| Disease | Key lab finding | 1st-line drug |
|---|---|---|
| T2DM | ↑ HbA1c/glucose | Metformin |
| T1DM | ↑ glucose, ketosis-prone | Insulin |
| Hypothyroidism | ↑TSH, ↓T4 | Levothyroxine |
| Hyperthyroidism | ↓TSH, ↑T4 | Carbimazole / PTU |

## Self-Test

<details>
<summary>Q1. Why is Metformin avoided in renal impairment?</summary>
Risk of lactic acidosis due to reduced renal clearance.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q2. Which antithyroid drug is preferred in the first trimester of pregnancy and why?</summary>
Propylthiouracil (PTU) — Carbimazole/Methimazole are associated with congenital malformations (aplasia cutis) in early pregnancy.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q3. What lab pattern confirms primary hypothyroidism?</summary>
Elevated TSH with low free T4.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q4. Name the most common cause of hyperthyroidism.</summary>
Graves' disease (autoimmune, TSH-receptor stimulating antibodies).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q5. What serious ADR must patients on Carbimazole be warned about?</summary>
Agranulocytosis — report sore throat/fever immediately.
</details>
