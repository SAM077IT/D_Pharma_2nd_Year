# Pharmacotherapeutics — Chapter 2(d)
## Central Nervous System: Epilepsy · Parkinson's Disease · Alzheimer's Disease · Stroke · Migraine

> **Weightage:** 8 hrs · **3-day plan:** Day 1 — last topic of the day

## 1. Epilepsy

**Definition:** Chronic neurological disorder characterized by recurrent, unprovoked seizures due to abnormal, excessive neuronal electrical discharge.

**Classification (high-yield):**
| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| Generalized tonic-clonic | Loss of consciousness, stiffening (tonic) then jerking (clonic) |
| Absence (petit mal) | Brief lapses of awareness, common in children |
| Focal/partial | Confined to one area; may or may not impair awareness |
| Status epilepticus | Seizure >5 min or repeated seizures without recovery — **medical emergency** |

**Etiopathogenesis:** Idiopathic/genetic, birth trauma, head injury, CNS infection, tumour, stroke, metabolic disturbance (hypoglycaemia, electrolyte imbalance), fever (febrile seizures in children).

**Clinical manifestations:** Depends on seizure type — loss of consciousness, tonic-clonic movements, tongue biting, incontinence, post-ictal confusion (generalized); brief staring spells (absence).

**Non-pharmacological management:** Adequate sleep, avoid triggers (alcohol, sleep deprivation, flashing lights), safety precautions, ketogenic diet (refractory paediatric epilepsy), surgery/vagal nerve stimulation in drug-resistant cases.

**Pharmacological management:**
| Seizure type | Drug of choice |
|---|---|
| Generalized tonic-clonic | Sodium valproate, Phenytoin, Carbamazepine |
| Absence seizures | Ethosuximide, Sodium valproate |
| Focal seizures | Carbamazepine, Phenytoin, Lamotrigine |
| Status epilepticus | IV Lorazepam/Diazepam → Phenytoin/Fosphenytoin loading |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Key monitoring for Phenytoin</summary>
Narrow therapeutic index — monitor serum levels (10–20 mcg/mL); ADRs: gingival hyperplasia, hirsutism, ataxia, nystagmus at toxic levels.
</details>

## 2. Parkinson's Disease (PD)

**Definition:** Progressive neurodegenerative disorder due to loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra.

**Clinical manifestations — mnemonic "TRAP":**
- **T**remor (resting, pill-rolling)
- **R**igidity (cogwheel)
- **A**kinesia/bradykinesia
- **P**ostural instability
- Additional: shuffling gait, mask-like facies, micrographia

**Non-pharmacological management:** Physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech therapy, fall-prevention measures, nutritional support (dysphagia risk).

**Pharmacological management:**
| Class | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dopamine precursor | **Levodopa + Carbidopa** | Mainstay/most effective; Carbidopa prevents peripheral breakdown |
| Dopamine agonists | Pramipexole, Ropinirole | Often used early in younger patients |
| MAO-B inhibitors | Selegiline | Mild symptomatic/neuroprotective |
| COMT inhibitors | Entacapone | Add-on, prolongs levodopa effect |
| Anticholinergics | Trihexyphenidyl | Useful for tremor, esp. in younger patients |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Why combine Levodopa with Carbidopa?</summary>
Carbidopa is a peripheral decarboxylase inhibitor — prevents peripheral conversion of levodopa to dopamine, reducing peripheral side effects (nausea, hypotension) and increasing levodopa availability to the brain.
</details>

## 3. Alzheimer's Disease

**Definition:** Progressive neurodegenerative disorder causing dementia, associated with amyloid-β plaques and neurofibrillary tangles, with cholinergic neuron loss.

**Clinical manifestations:** Progressive memory loss (short-term first), disorientation, language difficulty, impaired judgment, personality changes, eventually loss of ability to perform daily activities.

**Non-pharmacological management:** Cognitive stimulation, structured routine, caregiver support/education, safety modification of home environment.

**Pharmacological management:**
| Class | Examples | Stage used |
|---|---|---|
| Cholinesterase inhibitors | Donepezil, Rivastigmine, Galantamine | Mild-moderate disease |
| NMDA receptor antagonist | Memantine | Moderate-severe disease |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Mechanism of Memantine</summary>
NMDA receptor antagonist — reduces excitotoxicity from excess glutamate activity, used in moderate-to-severe Alzheimer's (can combine with a cholinesterase inhibitor).
</details>

## 4. Stroke

**Definition:** Sudden onset of neurological deficit due to disruption of cerebral blood supply.

| Type | Cause |
|---|---|
| Ischaemic (~85%) | Thrombosis/embolism blocking a cerebral vessel |
| Haemorrhagic (~15%) | Rupture of a cerebral vessel (intracerebral/subarachnoid) |

**Clinical manifestations — mnemonic "FAST":** **F**ace drooping, **A**rm weakness, **S**peech difficulty, **T**ime to call emergency services. Also: sudden vision loss, severe headache, confusion, loss of balance.

**Non-pharmacological management:** Risk factor control (BP, glucose, lipids), smoking cessation, physiotherapy/rehabilitation post-stroke, dysphagia screening.

**Pharmacological management:**
| Stage | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Acute ischaemic stroke | IV thrombolysis (Alteplase) within 4.5 hrs of onset, if no contraindication; mechanical thrombectomy in eligible large-vessel cases |
| Secondary prevention (ischaemic) | Antiplatelets (Aspirin, Clopidogrel), Statins, BP control |
| Haemorrhagic stroke | **Avoid** thrombolytics/antiplatelets; BP control, neurosurgical evaluation |
| AF-related stroke prevention | Anticoagulants (Warfarin, DOACs) |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Why must a CT scan precede thrombolysis in suspected stroke?</summary>
To differentiate ischaemic from haemorrhagic stroke — thrombolytics are contraindicated (fatal) in haemorrhagic stroke.
</details>

## 5. Migraine

**Definition:** Recurrent primary headache disorder, often unilateral, throbbing, associated with nausea and photo/phonophobia; may be preceded by an aura.

**Etiopathogenesis:** Trigeminovascular activation, cortical spreading depression (in migraine with aura), vasodilation of cranial vessels; triggers — stress, hormonal changes, certain foods (cheese, chocolate, caffeine), sleep disturbance.

**Clinical manifestations:** Unilateral throbbing headache (4–72 hrs), nausea/vomiting, photophobia, phonophobia; aura (visual disturbances) in ~20–30% of patients before headache onset.

**Non-pharmacological management:** Identify/avoid triggers, regular sleep schedule, stress management, adequate hydration, headache diary.

**Pharmacological management:**
| Purpose | Class | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Acute/abortive | NSAIDs | Ibuprofen, Naproxen (mild-moderate) |
| Acute/abortive | Triptans | Sumatriptan, Rizatriptan (moderate-severe, migraine-specific) |
| Acute (severe/refractory) | Ergot alkaloids | Ergotamine |
| Prophylaxis (frequent attacks) | Beta-blockers | Propranolol |
| Prophylaxis | Anticonvulsants | Topiramate, Sodium valproate |
| Prophylaxis | TCA | Amitriptyline |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Contraindication of Triptans</summary>
Contraindicated in patients with coronary artery disease/ischaemic heart disease and uncontrolled hypertension — triptans cause vasoconstriction (5-HT1B/1D agonists).
</details>

## Quick Revision Table

| Disease | Hallmark | 1st-line drug |
|---|---|---|
| Epilepsy (GTC) | Recurrent unprovoked seizures | Sodium valproate/Phenytoin |
| Parkinson's | TRAP (tremor, rigidity, akinesia, postural instability) | Levodopa + Carbidopa |
| Alzheimer's | Progressive memory loss | Donepezil (mild-mod); Memantine (mod-severe) |
| Stroke | Sudden focal neuro deficit (FAST) | Alteplase (if ischaemic, <4.5 hr) |
| Migraine | Unilateral throbbing headache ± aura | Triptans (acute) / Propranolol (prophylaxis) |

## Self-Test

<details>
<summary>Q1. What is status epilepticus and why is it an emergency?</summary>
Seizure lasting >5 min, or repeated seizures without recovery of consciousness — risk of permanent neuronal damage and death; treated with IV benzodiazepines then phenytoin/fosphenytoin.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q2. Expand the mnemonic TRAP for Parkinson's disease.</summary>
Tremor, Rigidity, Akinesia/bradykinesia, Postural instability.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q3. Which drug class is used for moderate-to-severe Alzheimer's disease, and what is its mechanism?</summary>
Memantine — NMDA receptor antagonist, reduces glutamate-mediated excitotoxicity.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q4. Why is a CT scan essential before giving thrombolytics in stroke?</summary>
To rule out haemorrhagic stroke, in which thrombolytics are contraindicated and can be fatal.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q5. Name the migraine-specific drug class contraindicated in ischaemic heart disease.</summary>
Triptans (e.g., Sumatriptan) — cause vasoconstriction.
</details>
