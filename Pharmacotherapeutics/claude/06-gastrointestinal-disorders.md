# Pharmacotherapeutics — Chapter 2(e)
## Gastro Intestinal Disorders: GERD · Peptic Ulcer Disease · Alcoholic Liver Disease · IBD

> **Weightage:** 8 hrs · **3-day plan:** Day 2

---

## 1. Gastro-oesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD)

**Definition:** Reflux of gastric contents into the oesophagus causing troublesome symptoms and/or mucosal injury, due to transient/incompetent lower oesophageal sphincter (LES) relaxation.

**Etiopathogenesis:** ↓ LES tone (obesity, pregnancy, smoking, fatty food, caffeine, alcohol), hiatus hernia, delayed gastric emptying.

**Clinical manifestations:** Heartburn (retrosternal burning, worse after meals/lying down), regurgitation, dysphagia, chronic cough, hoarseness; complication — Barrett's oesophagus (risk of malignancy) if untreated.

**Non-pharmacological management:** Weight loss, avoid late/large meals, elevate head of bed, avoid trigger foods (spicy, fatty, caffeine, alcohol), stop smoking.

**Pharmacological management:**
| Class | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| PPIs | **Omeprazole, Pantoprazole** | 1st line — most effective acid suppression |
| H2 blockers | Ranitidine (limited use now), Famotidine | Alternative/mild disease |
| Antacids | Mg/Al hydroxide | Quick symptomatic relief |
| Prokinetics | Domperidone, Metoclopramide | Add-on for delayed gastric emptying |

---

## 2. Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD)

**Definition:** Mucosal break in the stomach (gastric ulcer) or duodenum (duodenal ulcer) extending through the muscularis mucosa.

**Etiopathogenesis:** **H. pylori infection** (majority of cases), NSAID use, smoking, stress ulcers (critically ill), Zollinger-Ellison syndrome (rare, gastrin-secreting tumour).

**Clinical manifestations:** Epigastric pain — **duodenal ulcer pain relieved by food/worsens 2–3 hrs after meals & at night**; **gastric ulcer pain worsens with food**; nausea, bloating; complications — bleeding (melena/haematemesis), perforation.

**Non-pharmacological management:** Avoid NSAIDs/alcohol/smoking, stress reduction, small frequent meals.

**Pharmacological management:**
| Purpose | Class | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Acid suppression | PPIs | Omeprazole — mainstay of healing |
| H. pylori eradication | Triple therapy | PPI + Amoxicillin + Clarithromycin (7–14 days) |
| Mucosal protection | Sucralfate, Bismuth | Adjunct |
| NSAID-induced ulcer prevention | Misoprostol | Prostaglandin analogue, co-prescribed with NSAIDs in high-risk patients |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Standard H. pylori eradication regimen</summary>
Triple therapy for 7–14 days: PPI (e.g., Omeprazole) + Amoxicillin + Clarithromycin. (Metronidazole substituted for Amoxicillin in penicillin-allergic patients.)
</details>

## 3. Alcoholic Liver Disease (ALD)

**Definition:** Spectrum of liver damage from chronic alcohol use — fatty liver → alcoholic hepatitis → cirrhosis.

**Etiopathogenesis:** Chronic ethanol metabolism → acetaldehyde toxicity → hepatocyte injury, fat accumulation, inflammation, fibrosis over time.

**Clinical manifestations:** Hepatomegaly, jaundice, anorexia, fatigue; advanced disease — ascites, oedema, variceal bleeding, hepatic encephalopathy, spider angiomata, palmar erythema.

**Non-pharmacological management:** **Complete alcohol abstinence (cornerstone of management)**, nutritional support (thiamine/folate supplementation), management of complications (ascites — salt restriction).

**Pharmacological management:**
| Purpose | Drug |
|---|---|
| Alcoholic hepatitis (severe) | Corticosteroids (Prednisolone) — selected patients |
| Ascites | Spironolactone ± Furosemide |
| Hepatic encephalopathy | Lactulose, Rifaximin |
| Alcohol dependence support | Disulfiram, Naltrexone, Acamprosate |
| Nutritional | Thiamine (prevents Wernicke's encephalopathy), Folate |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Why give thiamine before glucose in chronic alcoholics?</summary>
Glucose administration without thiamine can precipitate Wernicke's encephalopathy in thiamine-deficient chronic alcoholics — thiamine should be given first/concurrently.
</details>

## 4. Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD): Crohn's Disease & Ulcerative Colitis

**Definition:** Chronic idiopathic inflammatory disorders of the GI tract — autoimmune-mediated.

**Key differentiator (exam favorite):**
| Feature | Crohn's Disease | Ulcerative Colitis |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Anywhere mouth to anus (skip lesions), commonly terminal ileum | Colon & rectum only, continuous |
| Depth | Transmural | Mucosal/submucosal only |
| Complications | Fistulas, strictures, abscesses | Toxic megacolon, ↑ colorectal cancer risk |
| Pathology | Cobblestone appearance, granulomas | Continuous ulceration, pseudopolyps |

**Clinical manifestations:** Abdominal pain, chronic diarrhoea (bloody in UC), weight loss, fatigue, fever; extraintestinal — arthritis, uveitis, erythema nodosum.

**Non-pharmacological management:** Nutritional support, avoid trigger foods, smoking cessation (worsens Crohn's; interestingly smoking may have a protective association in UC but cessation is still advised for overall health), psychological support.

**Pharmacological management:**
| Class | Examples | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Aminosalicylates | Sulfasalazine, Mesalamine | Mild-moderate UC (mainstay), maintenance |
| Corticosteroids | Prednisolone, Budesonide | Induce remission in flares (not for maintenance) |
| Immunomodulators | Azathioprine, Methotrexate | Steroid-sparing, maintenance |
| Biologics | Infliximab, Adalimumab (anti-TNF) | Moderate-severe/refractory disease, both CD & UC |
| Surgery | Colectomy | Curative in UC (colon removed); not curative in Crohn's |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Why is colectomy curative in UC but not Crohn's?</summary>
UC is confined to the colon/rectum, so removing the colon removes the diseased tissue entirely. Crohn's can affect any part of the GI tract, so disease can recur elsewhere even after surgery.
</details>

## Quick Revision Table

| Disease | Hallmark | 1st-line drug |
|---|---|---|
| GERD | Heartburn, LES incompetence | PPI (Omeprazole) |
| PUD | Epigastric pain, H. pylori | PPI + Amoxicillin + Clarithromycin |
| ALD | Chronic alcohol use, hepatomegaly | Abstinence + Thiamine |
| Crohn's/UC | Chronic GI inflammation | Aminosalicylates / Biologics |

## Self-Test

<details>
<summary>Q1. Duodenal ulcer pain typically does what in relation to meals?</summary>
Improves with food, worsens 2–3 hours after meals and at night (opposite of gastric ulcer, which worsens with food).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q2. Name the standard triple therapy for H. pylori eradication.</summary>
PPI + Amoxicillin + Clarithromycin for 7–14 days.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q3. What must be given before/with glucose in a malnourished chronic alcoholic and why?</summary>
Thiamine — to prevent precipitating Wernicke's encephalopathy.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q4. Distinguish Crohn's disease from Ulcerative colitis by depth of involvement.</summary>
Crohn's — transmural (full-thickness), can affect any GI segment with skip lesions. UC — mucosal/submucosal only, continuous, confined to colon and rectum.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q5. Which drug class is used for both Crohn's and UC in moderate-severe/refractory disease?</summary>
Biologics — anti-TNF agents like Infliximab, Adalimumab.
</details>
