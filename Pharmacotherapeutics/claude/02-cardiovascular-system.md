# Pharmacotherapeutics — Chapter 2(a)
## Cardiovascular System: Hypertension · Angina & MI · Hyperlipidaemia · CHF

> **Weightage:** 8 hrs · **3-day plan:** Day 1 · Highest-yield sub-topic in the syllabus

---

## 1. Hypertension (HTN)

**Definition:** Persistently elevated BP ≥140/90 mmHg (office); classified by JNC-8/ACC-AHA guidelines.

| Category | Systolic | Diastolic |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | <120 | <80 |
| Elevated | 120–129 | <80 |
| Stage 1 HTN | 130–139 | 80–89 |
| Stage 2 HTN | ≥140 | ≥90 |
| Hypertensive crisis | >180 | >120 |

**Etiopathogenesis:**
- **Primary/essential (90–95%)** — multifactorial: genetics, high salt intake, obesity, sympathetic overactivity, RAAS overactivity, endothelial dysfunction
- **Secondary (5–10%)** — renal disease, renal artery stenosis, pheochromocytoma, Cushing's syndrome, hyperaldosteronism, coarctation of aorta, drugs (NSAIDs, OCPs, steroids)

**Clinical manifestations:** Often asymptomatic ("silent killer"); headache (occipital, morning), dizziness, blurred vision, epistaxis; end-organ signs in advanced disease (retinopathy, LVH, nephropathy).

**Non-pharmacological management (DASH):**
- Salt restriction (<5–6 g/day)
- Weight reduction (BMI 18.5–24.9)
- DASH diet (fruits, vegetables, low-fat dairy)
- Regular aerobic exercise (≥30 min/day)
- Limit alcohol, stop smoking
- Stress management

**Pharmacological management — 5 first-line classes:**
| Class | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Thiazide diuretics | Hydrochlorothiazide, Chlorthalidone | 1st line, esp. in elderly |
| ACE inhibitors | Enalapril, Ramipril | Avoid in pregnancy, bilateral renal artery stenosis |
| ARBs | Losartan, Telmisartan | Alternative to ACEi (no cough) |
| CCBs | Amlodipine, Nifedipine | Good for elderly, isolated systolic HTN |
| Beta-blockers | Atenolol, Metoprolol | Not first-line alone now; preferred with concomitant CAD/HF |

**Compelling indications (exam favorite — match disease to drug class):**
- CHF → ACEi/ARB + Beta-blocker + diuretic
- Post-MI → Beta-blocker + ACEi
- Diabetes with proteinuria → ACEi/ARB
- Pregnancy → Methyldopa, Labetalol, Nifedipine (**never** ACEi/ARB)

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Why avoid ACEi/ARB in pregnancy?</summary>
Teratogenic — cause fetal renal damage, oligohydramnios, and skull ossification defects (2nd/3rd trimester).
</details>

## 2. Angina & Myocardial Infarction (MI)

**Angina — Definition:** Chest pain from myocardial ischaemia due to imbalance between O₂ supply and demand, usually from coronary atherosclerosis.

| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| Stable angina | Predictable, exertional, relieved by rest/nitrates |
| Unstable angina | At rest, worsening, high MI risk (ACS spectrum) |
| Variant (Prinzmetal's) | Coronary vasospasm, at rest, often at night |

**MI — Definition:** Irreversible myocardial necrosis due to prolonged ischaemia, usually from coronary plaque rupture + thrombosis.

**Clinical features of MI:** Severe crushing retrosternal chest pain (>20 min, not relieved by rest/nitrates), radiating to left arm/jaw, sweating, nausea, dyspnoea. Diagnosis: ECG changes (ST elevation/depression, Q waves) + cardiac biomarkers (Troponin I/T, CK-MB).

**Non-pharmacological management:** Smoking cessation, weight control, exercise (post-stabilization), low-fat/low-salt diet, cardiac rehabilitation.

**Pharmacological management of angina:**
| Class | Examples | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrates | Sublingual GTN, Isosorbide dinitrate | Symptom relief (venodilation ↓ preload) |
| Beta-blockers | Atenolol, Metoprolol | ↓ O₂ demand, first-line for stable angina |
| CCBs | Amlodipine, Diltiazem | Alternative/add-on, drug of choice in Prinzmetal's |
| Antiplatelets | Aspirin | Prevent thrombosis |
| Statins | Atorvastatin | Plaque stabilization |

**Acute MI management — mnemonic "MONA-BASH":**
- **M**orphine (pain relief)
- **O**xygen
- **N**itrates
- **A**spirin (+ P2Y12 inhibitor e.g. Clopidogrel — dual antiplatelet therapy)
- **B**eta-blockers
- **A**CE inhibitors
- **S**tatins (high-intensity)
- **H**eparin (anticoagulation) / thrombolytics (Streptokinase, Alteplase) if PCI unavailable within 90–120 min

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Absolute contraindication for thrombolytics?</summary>
Active internal bleeding, recent haemorrhagic stroke, recent major surgery/trauma, suspected aortic dissection — bleeding risk.
</details>

## 3. Hyperlipidaemia

**Definition:** Elevated plasma lipids — total cholesterol, LDL-C, triglycerides, and/or low HDL-C.

**Desirable levels (approx.):** Total cholesterol <200 mg/dL · LDL <100 mg/dL · HDL >40 mg/dL (men)/>50 (women) · TG <150 mg/dL

**Etiopathogenesis:** Primary (genetic — familial hypercholesterolaemia, LDL-receptor defect) or secondary (diabetes, hypothyroidism, obesity, alcohol, nephrotic syndrome, diet).

**Clinical features:** Usually asymptomatic; xanthomas, xanthelasma, arcus cornealis in severe/familial cases; consequence = atherosclerosis → CAD/stroke.

**Non-pharmacological management:** Low saturated-fat diet, increase fibre, weight loss, exercise, reduce alcohol, smoking cessation.

**Pharmacological management:**
| Class | Examples | Primary effect |
|---|---|---|
| Statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors) | Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin | ↓↓ LDL — **1st line** |
| Fibrates | Fenofibrate, Gemfibrozil | ↓↓ Triglycerides |
| Bile acid sequestrants | Cholestyramine | ↓ LDL |
| Niacin | Nicotinic acid | ↑ HDL, ↓ TG |
| PCSK9 inhibitors | Evolocumab, Alirocumab | ↓↓ LDL (add-on, expensive) |
| Ezetimibe | — | ↓ intestinal cholesterol absorption |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Key statin adverse effects to counsel on</summary>
Myopathy/rhabdomyolysis (report muscle pain), hepatotoxicity (monitor LFTs), avoid grapefruit juice with some statins (CYP3A4 interaction), avoid in pregnancy.
</details>

## 4. Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)

**Definition:** Clinical syndrome where the heart cannot pump sufficient blood to meet metabolic demands, or does so only at elevated filling pressures.

**NYHA Functional Classification (exam favorite):**
| Class | Symptom level |
|---|---|
| I | No limitation of physical activity |
| II | Slight limitation; comfortable at rest |
| III | Marked limitation; comfortable only at rest |
| IV | Symptoms at rest; any activity increases discomfort |

**Etiopathogenesis:** CAD/MI, hypertension, valvular disease, cardiomyopathy → ↓ cardiac output → compensatory RAAS + sympathetic activation → fluid retention & remodeling (worsens HF over time).

**Clinical manifestations:** Dyspnoea (exertional → orthopnoea → PND), fatigue, bilateral pedal oedema, raised JVP, hepatomegaly, basal crepitations.

**Non-pharmacological management:** Salt & fluid restriction, daily weight monitoring, graded exercise, vaccination (flu/pneumococcal), avoid NSAIDs.

**Pharmacological management (mortality-benefit drugs first):**
| Class | Examples | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| ACEi/ARB | Enalapril, Losartan | ↓ mortality, afterload reduction |
| Beta-blockers | Carvedilol, Bisoprolol | ↓ mortality (start low, go slow) |
| Mineralocorticoid antagonists | Spironolactone, Eplerenone | ↓ mortality in advanced HF |
| Loop diuretics | Furosemide | Symptom relief (fluid overload) — no mortality benefit |
| Digoxin | — | Symptom control, ↓ hospitalization, narrow therapeutic index |
| SGLT2 inhibitors | Dapagliflozin | Newer addition, mortality benefit even in non-diabetics |

<details>
<summary>💡 High-yield: Digoxin toxicity signs</summary>
Nausea/vomiting, visual disturbances (yellow-green halos/xanthopsia), arrhythmias (esp. with hypokalaemia). Antidote: Digoxin immune Fab (Digibind).
</details>

## Self-Test

<details>
<summary>Q1. What BP defines Stage 2 hypertension?</summary>
≥140/90 mmHg.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q2. Which antihypertensives are contraindicated in pregnancy and why?</summary>
ACE inhibitors and ARBs — teratogenic, cause fetal renal damage and oligohydramnios.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q3. Expand MONA-BASH.</summary>
Morphine, Oxygen, Nitrates, Aspirin — Beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, Statins, Heparin/thrombolytics.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q4. Drug of choice for Prinzmetal's (variant) angina?</summary>
Calcium channel blockers (e.g., Diltiazem) — because it's caused by coronary vasospasm, not fixed obstruction.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q5. Which class of anti-lipid drugs is first-line for LDL reduction?</summary>
Statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q6. List the three drug classes proven to reduce mortality in CHF.</summary>
ACEi/ARB, Beta-blockers, Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (Spironolactone/Eplerenone) — plus SGLT2 inhibitors more recently.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q7. Classify a patient with heart failure symptoms only during heavy exertion, comfortable at rest, per NYHA.</summary>
NYHA Class II.
</details>
