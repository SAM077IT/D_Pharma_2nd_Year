# Chapter 4 — Endocrine Disorders
**Diabetes and Thyroid Disorders | 5 hours**

## A. Diabetes Mellitus

### Definition
A group of metabolic disorders characterized by persistent hyperglycaemia due to inadequate insulin secretion, impaired insulin action, or both.

### Common symptoms
Polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, weight change, fatigue, blurred vision and recurrent infections.

### Complications
- Retinopathy
- Nephropathy
- Neuropathy
- Cardiovascular disease
- Foot complications

### Management
**Lifestyle:** diet, physical activity, weight management, tobacco cessation, education and monitoring.

**Medicines:** metformin is commonly used in type 2 diabetes; other classes include sulfonylureas, DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists and insulin, selected according to patient factors and guideline.

### Hypoglycaemia
Typical symptoms: sweating, tremor, hunger, palpitations, confusion.

Immediate treatment for a conscious patient generally involves fast-acting carbohydrate, followed by reassessment and further food if appropriate.

## B. Hypothyroidism

### Features
Fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, constipation, dry skin and slow pulse may occur.

### Treatment
**Levothyroxine** is standard replacement therapy.

## C. Hyperthyroidism

### Features
Weight loss, heat intolerance, sweating, tremor, anxiety, palpitations and increased bowel frequency may occur.

### Treatment options
- Antithyroid drugs such as methimazole/carbimazole
- Beta-blocker for symptom control when appropriate
- Radioiodine or surgery in selected cases

### Important adverse effect
Antithyroid drugs can rarely cause agranulocytosis. Fever/sore throat during therapy requires urgent medical evaluation.
