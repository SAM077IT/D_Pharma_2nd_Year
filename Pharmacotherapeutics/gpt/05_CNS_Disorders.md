# Chapter 5 — Central Nervous System Disorders
**Epilepsy, Parkinson's disease, Alzheimer's disease, Stroke, Migraine | 8 hours**

## A. Epilepsy
A neurological disorder characterized by recurrent unprovoked seizures.

### Treatment
Antiseizure medicine is selected according to seizure type, age, comorbidity and other factors.

Examples:
- Sodium valproate
- Carbamazepine
- Lamotrigine
- Levetiracetam
- Ethosuximide for selected absence seizures

### Counselling
- Take medicines regularly
- Do not stop abruptly without medical advice
- Maintain sleep and avoid known triggers
- Follow driving/safety regulations
- Pregnancy planning requires specialist advice

## B. Parkinson's Disease
Progressive neurological disorder involving dopaminergic dysfunction.

### Cardinal features
- Resting tremor
- Rigidity
- Bradykinesia
- Postural instability

### Drugs
- Levodopa + carbidopa/benserazide
- Dopamine agonists
- MAO-B inhibitors
- COMT inhibitors
- Amantadine in selected patients

## C. Alzheimer's Disease
Progressive neurocognitive disorder causing memory and cognitive decline.

### Medicines
- Cholinesterase inhibitors: donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine
- Memantine in appropriate stages/patients

These medicines are symptomatic; they do not cure Alzheimer's disease.

## D. Stroke
Acute neurological deficit caused by interruption of brain blood flow (ischaemic) or bleeding (haemorrhagic).

### Risk factors
Hypertension, diabetes, smoking, atrial fibrillation, dyslipidaemia and age.

### Management
Emergency assessment, brain imaging, reperfusion when eligible for ischaemic stroke, secondary prevention and rehabilitation.

## E. Migraine
Recurrent headache disorder often associated with nausea and sensitivity to light/sound.

### Acute treatment
Paracetamol/NSAIDs and migraine-specific medicines such as triptans in suitable patients.

### Prevention
Consider preventive therapy for frequent/disabling attacks; options depend on patient factors.
