# Chapter 2 — Cardiovascular Disorders
**Syllabus: Hypertension, Angina & MI, Hyperlipidaemia, Congestive Heart Failure | 8 hours**

## A. Hypertension

### Definition
Persistent elevation of arterial blood pressure. Diagnosis and treatment thresholds depend on the guideline and patient risk profile.

### Risk factors
Age, obesity, high salt intake, sedentary lifestyle, tobacco, alcohol excess, diabetes, family history and kidney disease.

### Clinical features
Often asymptomatic. Possible headache, dizziness, visual symptoms, chest discomfort or complications.

### Non-pharmacological management
- Weight reduction when appropriate
- Salt restriction
- Regular physical activity
- Healthy diet
- Stop tobacco
- Moderate alcohol
- Stress/sleep management

### Drug classes
- ACE inhibitors: e.g. enalapril
- ARBs: e.g. losartan
- Calcium-channel blockers: e.g. amlodipine
- Thiazide/thiazide-like diuretics
- Beta-blockers in selected indications

### Important adverse effects
ACE inhibitors → cough, hyperkalaemia, angioedema  
ARBs → hyperkalaemia; generally no ACE-inhibitor cough  
CCBs → ankle oedema, flushing  
Thiazides → electrolyte disturbances  
Beta-blockers → bradycardia, fatigue; may mask hypoglycaemia

## B. Angina Pectoris
Chest discomfort caused by myocardial ischaemia, usually from imbalance between myocardial oxygen demand and supply.

### Management
- Risk-factor modification
- Antiplatelet therapy when indicated
- Statin therapy when indicated
- Nitrates for symptom relief
- Beta-blockers / calcium-channel blockers depending on patient
- Revascularization in selected patients

**Nitrates:** increase nitric oxide signalling → vasodilation → reduced cardiac preload and oxygen demand.

### Important counselling
Do **not** combine nitrates with PDE-5 inhibitors such as sildenafil because of potentially dangerous hypotension.

## C. Myocardial Infarction
MI is myocardial necrosis caused by prolonged interruption of coronary blood flow, commonly due to acute coronary thrombosis.

### Key symptoms
- Prolonged central chest pressure/pain
- Radiation to arm/jaw/back
- Sweating
- Nausea
- Dyspnoea

### Treatment principles
Urgent emergency assessment and reperfusion when indicated, plus antiplatelet/anticoagulant and secondary-prevention therapy according to the clinical situation.

## D. Hyperlipidaemia
Abnormally elevated blood lipids, particularly LDL cholesterol.

### Management
Diet, exercise, weight management + lipid-lowering therapy where indicated.

**Statins** inhibit HMG-CoA reductase and lower LDL cholesterol.

Important adverse effect: muscle symptoms; rare severe muscle injury.

## E. Congestive Heart Failure
A clinical syndrome in which the heart cannot adequately meet the body's demands without elevated filling pressures.

### Features
Dyspnoea, fatigue, peripheral oedema, orthopnoea, pulmonary congestion.

### Management principles
- Salt/fluid advice where appropriate
- Diuretics for congestion
- Evidence-based disease-modifying therapy according to heart-failure phenotype
- Treat underlying cause
- Patient monitoring
