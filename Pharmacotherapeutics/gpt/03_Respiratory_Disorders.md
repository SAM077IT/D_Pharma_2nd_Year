# Chapter 3 — Respiratory Disorders
**Asthma and COPD | 4 hours**

## A. Asthma

### Definition
A chronic inflammatory airway disorder characterized by variable respiratory symptoms and variable expiratory airflow limitation.

### Symptoms
- Wheezing
- Breathlessness
- Chest tightness
- Cough
Symptoms may vary over time and intensity.

### Triggers
Allergens, smoke, exercise, infections, occupational exposures and some medicines.

### Non-pharmacological management
- Avoid individual triggers
- Stop smoking/exposure to smoke
- Vaccination where appropriate
- Written asthma action plan
- Correct inhaler technique
- Regular review

### Pharmacological management
- Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS): foundation of anti-inflammatory treatment
- ICS + LABA in appropriate patients
- Reliever therapy according to current guideline
- Leukotriene receptor antagonists in selected cases

**Key point:** Bronchodilators relieve bronchoconstriction; corticosteroids control airway inflammation.

## B. COPD

### Definition
A chronic respiratory disease characterized by persistent respiratory symptoms and airflow limitation, usually related to airway/alveolar abnormalities and significant exposure to noxious particles or gases.

### Symptoms
- Chronic cough
- Sputum production
- Dyspnoea
- Reduced exercise tolerance

### Management
- Smoking cessation
- Vaccination
- Pulmonary rehabilitation
- Bronchodilators
- ICS for selected patients
- Oxygen in selected patients with chronic hypoxaemia

### Asthma vs COPD
| Feature | Asthma | COPD |
|---|---|---|
| Variability | Usually marked | Usually persistent |
| Common association | Allergy/airway hyperresponsiveness | Smoking/exposure |
| Main controller concept | Anti-inflammatory therapy | Bronchodilation + risk-factor control |
| Reversibility | Often substantial | Usually limited |

## Inhaler counselling
1. Demonstrate device
2. Patient repeats technique
3. Check technique at follow-up
4. Explain dose/frequency
5. Rinse mouth after inhaled corticosteroid use

## Quick Recall
<details><summary>Controller vs reliever?</summary>Controller therapy reduces underlying airway inflammation/risk; reliever therapy rapidly improves symptoms/bronchoconstriction.</details>

<details><summary>Most important lifestyle intervention in COPD?</summary>Smoking cessation when smoking is a cause/exposure.</details>
