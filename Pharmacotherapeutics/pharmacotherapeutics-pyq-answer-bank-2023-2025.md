## SECTION A — Long & Short Answer Bank (merge of 3-mark and 5-mark questions, by topic)

### 1. 🔥 Pharmacotherapeutics — Definition, Scope & Objectives
*(Asked in: 2025 paper Q1, BTEUP Q1, ER20-24T Q4, 2024-PI Part B Q1)*
- **Definition:** Branch dealing with the use of drugs in treatment of disease — selecting the right drug/dose/route/duration for a patient.
- **Scope:** Assessing patient signs/symptoms, identifying drug-related problems, rational therapy planning, discharge counselling, monitoring outcomes.
- **Objectives:** Ensure safe & effective drug therapy; individualize treatment per patient factors (age, renal/hepatic status); reduce ADRs/drug interactions; promote cost-effective, evidence-based prescribing.

### 2. 🔥 Hypertension
*(Asked in: 2024-PI Part A Q1, BTEUP Q2, GTU "20224" — lifestyle Q, ER20-24T Part C Q29)*
- **Definition:** Persistently raised BP ≥140/90 mmHg.
- **Etiology:** Primary/essential (90–95%, genetic + lifestyle) or Secondary (renal disease, pheochromocytoma, Cushing's, drugs).
- **Clinical manifestations:** Mostly asymptomatic; headache, dizziness, blurred vision; end-organ damage if longstanding.
- **Lifestyle changes:** Salt restriction, weight loss, DASH diet, regular exercise, limit alcohol, stop smoking.
- **Pharmacological management:** Thiazide diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, CCBs, Beta-blockers (compelling indications: CHF→ACEi+BB; pregnancy→Methyldopa/Labetalol, never ACEi/ARB).

### 3. 🔥 Diabetes Mellitus
*(Asked in: 2024-PI Q3, BTEUP Q3, GTU "20224" Q1c, ER20-24T Part B Q18, 2025-PI Part B Q10)*
- **Types:** Type 1 (autoimmune β-cell destruction, absolute insulin deficiency), Type 2 (insulin resistance, majority of cases), Gestational.
- **Etiopathogenesis:** Genetic + obesity/lifestyle (Type 2); autoimmune (Type 1).
- **Clinical manifestations:** Polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, recurrent infections, delayed wound healing.
- **Management:** Diet/exercise/SMBG (non-pharm); Metformin (1st-line T2DM), Sulfonylureas, DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors, Insulin (mandatory in T1DM).

### 4. Asthma
*(Asked in: 2024-PI Part A Q2, GTU "20224" Q2b)*
- **Definition:** Chronic inflammatory airway disease with reversible obstruction & hyperresponsiveness.
- **Etiopathogenesis:** Allergens, infection, exercise, cold air → Type I hypersensitivity → bronchoconstriction.
- **Symptoms/Clinical manifestations:** Episodic wheezing, breathlessness, chest tightness, nocturnal dry cough.
- **Treatment:** SABA (Salbutamol) for quick relief; ICS (Budesonide) as controller; LABA add-on (never alone); Leukotriene antagonist (Montelukast); severe — oral steroids.

### 5. 🔥 Malaria
*(Asked in: BTEUP Q5, GTU "20224" Q2f, ER20-24T Part A Q1, 2025-PI Part B Q2)*
- **Etiopathogenesis:** Mosquito-borne (female Anopheles), caused by *Plasmodium* species (vivax, falciparum).
- **Clinical manifestations:** Cyclical fever with chills/rigors, headache, myalgia, splenomegaly; falciparum → cerebral malaria/severe anaemia (complicated).
- **Non-pharm management:** Vector control, mosquito nets, indoor spraying.
- **Pharm management:** *P. vivax* — Chloroquine + Primaquine (radical cure, check G6PD first); *P. falciparum* — ACT (Artemisinin Combination Therapy); severe — IV Artesunate.

### 6. 🔥 Peptic Ulcer Disease
*(Asked in: GTU "20224" Q1e, BTEUP Q4, 2024-PI Part B Q2 — symptoms, 2025-PI Part A Q4)*
- **Definition:** Mucosal break in stomach/duodenum.
- **Etiopathogenesis:** *H. pylori* (majority), NSAID use, smoking, stress.
- **Symptoms/Clinical manifestations:** Epigastric pain — duodenal ulcer relieved by food/worse at night; gastric ulcer worse with food; nausea, bloating; complications — bleeding, perforation.
- **Management:** PPI (Omeprazole) mainstay; H. pylori eradication — PPI + Amoxicillin + Clarithromycin (7–14 days); Misoprostol to prevent NSAID-induced ulcers.

### 7. 🔥 Psoriasis
*(Asked in: BTEUP Q6, 2024-PI Part B Q3 — treatment, ER20-24T Part B Q17 — etiopathogenesis, BTEUP Part C Q14 — Auspitz sign)*
- **Definition:** Chronic immune-mediated skin disorder — hyperproliferation of keratinocytes.
- **Etiopathogenesis:** T-cell autoimmune process → accelerated epidermal turnover; triggers — stress, infection, trauma (Koebner phenomenon), drugs.
- **Clinical manifestations:** Well-demarcated plaques with silvery-white scales on extensor surfaces; **Auspitz sign** = pinpoint bleeding on scale removal; nail pitting.
- **Treatment:** Topical corticosteroids, Vitamin D analogues (Calcipotriol), Coal tar (mild); Phototherapy, Methotrexate (moderate-severe); Biologics (severe).

### 8. 🔥 Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)
*(Asked in: BTEUP Q7, 2024-PI Part B Q4, GTU "20224" Q2j — mechanisms, ER20-24T Part B Q14 — causes, ER20-24T Part C Q34/35)*
- **Definition:** Ability of microorganisms to survive despite drug exposure that would normally kill/inhibit them.
- **Mechanisms:** Enzymatic drug inactivation (β-lactamase), altered target site, reduced drug uptake, efflux pumps, alternative metabolic pathways.
- **Causes:** Antibiotic overuse/misuse, incomplete courses, agricultural use, poor infection control, self-medication.
- **Prevention/control:** Antimicrobial stewardship (right drug/dose/duration), complete full course, infection control, surveillance, restrict OTC antibiotic sale, WHO Global Action Plan (2015) — 5 objectives (awareness, surveillance, infection prevention, optimize use, sustainable investment).
- Microbes that develop resistance = **Superbugs**.

### 9. Alcoholic Liver Disease
*(Asked in: 2024-PI Part A Q5, ER20-24T Part B Q13)*
- **Definition:** Spectrum of liver damage from chronic alcohol use — fatty liver → hepatitis → cirrhosis.
- **Pathogenesis:** Chronic ethanol metabolism → acetaldehyde toxicity → hepatocyte injury, fat accumulation, fibrosis.
- **Clinical manifestations:** Hepatomegaly, jaundice, anorexia, fatigue; advanced — ascites, variceal bleeding, encephalopathy.
- **Treatment:** Complete alcohol abstinence (cornerstone); Thiamine/folate supplementation (give thiamine before glucose — prevents Wernicke's); Spironolactone for ascites; Lactulose for encephalopathy.

### 10. 🔥 Rheumatoid Arthritis
*(Asked in: 2024-PI Part A Q6, BTEUP Part B Q9 — pharmacological treatment, ER20-24T Part C Q25)*
- **Definition:** Chronic autoimmune inflammatory disease of synovial joints, symmetric.
- **Clinical manifestations:** Symmetric polyarthritis (small joints), morning stiffness **>1 hour**, joint swelling, fatigue; RF & Anti-CCP positive.
- **Treatment:** Methotrexate (1st-line DMARD, + folic acid); Biologics (Etanercept, Adalimumab) for severe; NSAIDs for symptom relief; Corticosteroids as bridge therapy.
- Note: RA does **not** occur due to Vitamin D deficiency, and there is **no cure** — it's a chronic autoimmune disease.

### 11. 🔥 Iron Deficiency Anaemia
*(Asked in: 2024-PI Part A Q7, 2025-PI Part A Q7)*
- **Etiopathogenesis:** Chronic blood loss (menorrhagia, GI bleeding — most common), poor diet, malabsorption, increased demand (pregnancy).
- **Clinical manifestations:** Fatigue, pallor, koilonychia (spoon nails), pica, glossitis.
- **Treatment:** Oral ferrous sulfate (1st line, continue 3–6 months after Hb normalizes); Parenteral iron if intolerant/severe; Vitamin C aids absorption.

### 12. Evidence-Based Medicine (EBM)
*(Asked in: BTEUP Part B Q1)*
- **Definition:** Conscientious use of current best evidence in patient care decisions.
- **3 pillars:** Best research evidence + Clinical expertise + Patient values.
- **Importance:** Standardizes quality of care, reduces irrational prescribing, improves outcomes, forms basis of STGs/EML.

### 13. 🔥 Hyperlipidaemia
*(Asked in: BTEUP Part B Q2, ER20-24T Part B Q11, 2025-PI Part A Q2 — in detail)*
- **Definition:** Elevated total cholesterol, LDL-C, triglycerides and/or low HDL-C.
- **Types:** Primary (genetic — familial hypercholesterolaemia) or Secondary (diabetes, hypothyroidism, obesity, alcohol).
- **Clinical manifestations:** Usually asymptomatic; xanthomas, xanthelasma in severe cases; leads to atherosclerosis/CAD.
- **Management:** Diet/exercise (non-pharm); Statins (1st-line, ↓LDL), Fibrates (↓TG), Ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors.

### 14. Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)
*(Asked in: BTEUP Part B Q3, ER20-24T Part B Q10)*
- **Etiopathogenesis:** Chronic bronchitis (mucus gland hypertrophy) + Emphysema (alveolar wall destruction); main cause — **smoking**.
- **Clinical manifestations:** Chronic productive cough, progressive exertional dyspnoea, wheeze, barrel chest.
- **Management:** Smoking cessation (most effective), LAMA (Tiotropium)/LABA maintenance, ICS in frequent exacerbators, antibiotics during infective exacerbation.

### 15. 🔥 Hyperthyroidism & Hypothyroidism
*(Hyperthyroidism asked: BTEUP Part B Q4; Hypothyroidism 🔥 asked: 2024-PI Part B Q10, 2025-PI Part B Q6, ER20-24T Part C Q31/38, BTEUP Part C Q4)*
- **Hypothyroidism:** ↓T3/T4. Cause: Hashimoto's thyroiditis (most common), iodine deficiency. Symptoms: fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, bradycardia, **myxoedema**. Labs: ↑TSH, ↓T4. **Treatment: Levothyroxine (first choice).**
- **Hyperthyroidism:** ↑T3/T4. Cause: Graves' disease (most common). Symptoms: weight loss, heat intolerance, tachycardia, tremors, exophthalmos. Labs: ↓TSH, ↑T4. Treatment: Carbimazole/PTU, Beta-blockers for symptom control, Radioactive iodine.

### 16. 🔥 Alzheimer's Disease
*(Asked in: BTEUP Part B Q5 — four stages, 2025-PI Part B Q8 — short note)*
- **Definition:** Progressive neurodegenerative dementia — amyloid-β plaques, neurofibrillary tangles, cholinergic neuron loss.
- **Four stages:** (1) Preclinical — no symptoms; (2) Mild — short-term memory loss, mild confusion; (3) Moderate — increased memory loss, disorientation, personality changes, needs help with daily tasks; (4) Severe — loss of communication, complete dependence on caregivers.
- **Treatment:** Donepezil/Rivastigmine (cholinesterase inhibitors — mild-moderate); Memantine (NMDA antagonist — moderate-severe).

### 17. 🔥 GERD
*(Asked in: GTU "20224" Q2d, BTEUP Part B Q6, ER20-24T Part A Q6)*
- **Definition:** Reflux of gastric contents into oesophagus due to incompetent lower oesophageal sphincter (LES).
- **Etiology:** ↓LES tone (obesity, smoking, fatty food, caffeine, alcohol), hiatus hernia (a known risk factor).
- **Clinical manifestations:** Heartburn, regurgitation, dysphagia, chronic cough.
- **Pharmacological management:** PPI (Omeprazole) — 1st line; H2 blockers, Antacids for quick relief; Prokinetics as add-on.

### 18. 🔥 Megaloblastic Anaemia
*(Asked in: BTEUP Part B Q7, GTU "20224" Q2e, ER20-24T Part C Q23)*
- **Definition:** Impaired DNA synthesis → large immature RBC precursors, from **Vitamin B12 and/or Folate (Vitamin B9) deficiency**.
- **Clinical manifestations:** Fatigue, pallor, glossitis; B12 deficiency specifically → neurological symptoms (peripheral neuropathy, subacute combined degeneration).
- **Management:** IM/SC Vitamin B12 (Cyanocobalamin) for B12 deficiency; Oral Folic acid for folate deficiency — **must rule out B12 deficiency before giving folate alone** (masks anaemia but neurological damage progresses).

### 19. 🔥 COVID-19 / SARS-CoV-2
*(Asked in: 2024-PI Part A Q4, BTEUP Part B Q8, 2025-PI Part B Q4, GTU/ER20 Part C — causative agent)*
- **Causative agent:** SARS-CoV-2.
- **Symptoms:** Fever, cough, loss of taste/smell (anosmia — characteristic), sore throat, fatigue, dyspnoea in severe cases.
- **Non-pharmacological management:** Isolation, vaccination, hand hygiene, masking, supportive care (rest, hydration, antipyretics), oxygen/proning if hypoxic.
- **Approved vaccines in India:** Covishield (AstraZeneca), Covaxin, Sputnik V.

### 20. Psychosis / Psychoses
*(Asked in: ER20-24T Part A Q3, BTEUP Part B Q10)*
- **Definition:** Severe mental disorder — break from reality (hallucinations, delusions, disorganized thinking).
- **Clinical manifestations:** Positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions, disorganized speech) and Negative symptoms (flat affect, social withdrawal, avolition).
- **Etiopathogenesis:** Dopamine hypothesis — hyperactivity of mesolimbic dopaminergic pathway.

### 21. 🔥 Dysmenorrhoea
*(Asked in: BTEUP Part B Q11, GTU "20224" Q2k, BTEUP Part C Q19)*
- **Definition:** Painful menstrual cramps (pain associated with menstruation).
- **Types:** Primary (no pathology, excess prostaglandin PGF2α) vs Secondary (endometriosis, fibroids).
- **Management:** NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Mefenamic acid) — 1st line, target prostaglandin synthesis; Combined oral contraceptives as alternative.
- Note: Absence of periods = **Amenorrhoea** (different term, also asked as fill-blank).

### 22. 🔥 PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome)
*(Asked in: ER20-24T Part A Q2, 2025-PI Part B Q5, GTU "20224" — hyperandrogenism, ER20-24T Part C Q36)*
- **Diagnosis (Rotterdam, any 2 of 3):** Oligo/anovulation, hyperandrogenism, polycystic ovaries on USG.
- **Etiopathogenesis:** Insulin resistance (central) → ↑ovarian androgen production. **Hyperandrogenism is associated with PCOS.**
- **Clinical manifestations:** Irregular menses, hirsutism, acne, obesity, infertility.
- **Management:** Weight loss (1st line), Metformin (insulin resistance), COCs (cycle regulation/hirsutism), Clomiphene/Letrozole (ovulation induction if seeking pregnancy).

### 23. Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)
*(Asked in: ER20-24T Part A Q5)*
- **Pharmacological management:** ACEi/ARB, Beta-blockers, Spironolactone (all reduce mortality); Loop diuretics (Furosemide) for symptom relief; Digoxin for symptom control.
- **Non-pharmacological management:** Salt/fluid restriction, daily weight monitoring, graded exercise, vaccination.

### 24. 🔥 Parkinson's Disease
*(Asked in: ER20-24T Part A Q7, GTU "20224" Q2c — management, BTEUP Part C Q5, 2023 Part C Q5)*
- **Etiopathogenesis:** Loss of dopaminergic neurons in substantia nigra.
- **Clinical manifestations (TRAP):** Tremor (resting), Rigidity (cogwheel), Akinesia/bradykinesia, Postural instability.
- **Pharmacological management:** Levodopa + Carbidopa (mainstay); Dopamine agonists (Pramipexole); MAO-B inhibitors (Selegiline); Anticholinergics for tremor.

### 25. 🔥 Depression
*(Asked in: 2025-PI Part A Q6, 2024-PI Part B Q5 — pharmacological treatment, ER20-24T Part C Q28)*
- **Etiopathogenesis:** Monoamine hypothesis — **decreased levels of serotonin, norepinephrine, dopamine**.
- **Clinical manifestations:** Persistent sadness, anhedonia (**loss of interest in all things**), sleep/appetite change, fatigue, poor concentration, suicidal ideation.
- **Pharmacological management:** SSRIs (Fluoxetine, Sertraline) — 1st line; SNRIs; TCAs (Amitriptyline — older); MAOIs (reserved).

### 26. Hepatitis
*(Asked in: 2025-PI Part A Q5, GTU "20224" Q1f — types)*
- **Types:** Hep A/E — faeco-oral, self-limiting; Hep B/C — blood-borne, can become chronic.
- **Clinical manifestations:** Jaundice, anorexia, malaise, RUQ pain, dark urine.
- **Management:** Hep A/E — supportive; Hep B (chronic) — Tenofovir/Entecavir; Hep C — Sofosbuvir-based DAAs.

### 27. Epilepsy
*(Asked in: GTU "20224" Q1d — types, 2025-PI Part B Q11 — short note)*
- **Types:** Generalized tonic-clonic, Absence (petit mal), Focal, Status epilepticus (emergency).
- **Pharmacological management:** GTC — Sodium valproate/Phenytoin; Absence — Ethosuximide; Status epilepticus — IV Diazepam then Phenytoin loading.

### 28. Tuberculosis
*(Asked in: GTU "20224" Q1g — DOTS)*
- **DOTS/Management:** RIPE regimen — Rifampicin, Isoniazid, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol for 2 months (intensive) + Rifampicin+Isoniazid for 4 months (continuation); directly observed to ensure adherence. Isoniazid + Pyridoxine (prevent neuropathy). TB = **Koch's disease**.

### 29. 🔥 Angina
*(Asked in: 2024-PI Part B Q11 — short note, GTU "20224" Q1b — etiopathogenesis, BTEUP Part C Q1/2)*
- **Definition:** Chest pain from myocardial ischaemia (O2 supply-demand imbalance).
- **Etiopathogenesis:** Coronary atherosclerosis; **Stable angina** = provoked by exertion/emotional stress, relieved by rest.
- **Pharmacological management:** Nitrates (symptom relief), Beta-blockers (1st line), CCBs; Aspirin + Statins for secondary prevention.
- Cardiovascular disease affects the **heart and blood vessels**.

### 30. Conjunctivitis
*(Asked in: GTU "20224" Q2i, ER20-24T Part B Q8)*
- **Definition:** Inflammation of the conjunctiva.
- **Symptoms:** Bacterial — thick purulent discharge; Viral — watery discharge, often with cold symptoms.
- **Management:** Bacterial — topical antibiotics (Chloramphenicol/Ofloxacin); Viral — supportive only.

### 31. Osteoarthritis
*(Asked in: GTU "20224" Q2g, ER20-24T Part B Q16, 2023-BTEUP Part C Q12)*
- **Definition:** Degenerative "wear and tear" joint disease — cartilage breakdown; also called **Degenerative joint disease / Degenerative arthritis**.
- **Clinical manifestations:** Pain worse with activity, morning stiffness **<30 min**, Heberden's/Bouchard's nodes.
- **Non-pharmacological management:** Weight reduction, low-impact exercise, physiotherapy, assistive devices.

### 32. Anxiety
*(Asked in: GTU "20224" Q2h)*
- **Clinical manifestations:** Excessive worry, restlessness, muscle tension, poor concentration, sleep disturbance; panic attacks — palpitations, chest tightness, fear of dying.
- (Anxiety is a psychiatric disorder — distinguish from cardiovascular/respiratory diseases in MCQs.)

### 33. 🔥 Glaucoma
*(Asked in: 2024-PI Part B Q8, 2025-PI Part B Q9, GTU/ER20 Part C — disease of optic nerve, BTEUP Part C Q18)*
- **Definition:** Group of disorders with progressive **optic nerve** damage, usually with raised IOP.
- **Treatment:** Pilocarpine (↑outflow), Timolol (↓production), Latanoprost (prostaglandin analogue — 1st line open-angle), Acetazolamide.
- Angle-closure glaucoma = emergency (acute pain, halos, red eye).

### 34. Scabies
*(Asked in: 2025-PI Part B Q1, GTU "20224" — causative organism, BTEUP Part C Q14/15)*
- **Causative organism:** *Sarcoptes scabiei* (mite).
- **Symptoms:** Intense itching (worse at night), burrows in finger webs/wrists.
- **First-line treatment:** **Permethrin 5% cream.**

### 35. HIV
*(Asked in: 2025-PI Part B Q3, 2024-PI Part B Q6 — non-pharm measures)*
- **Most severe stage:** AIDS.
- **Non-pharmacological measures to avoid HIV/opportunistic infections:** Safe sex practices, needle-exchange programs, prevention of mother-to-child transmission, nutritional support, avoid exposure to infected fluids.
- **Most common opportunistic infection in HIV in India: Tuberculosis.**

### 36. Essential Medicine List / NLEM
*(Asked in: 2025-PI Part B Q7, ER20-24T Part B Q12, GTU "20224" — define)*
- **Definition:** List of medicines satisfying priority healthcare needs, selected for efficacy, safety, cost-effectiveness.
- **NLEM (India):** Latest is NLEM 2022. **Three aspects of NLEM: Cost, Efficacy, Safety.**
- **Updated:** WHO Model List revised **every 2 years.**

### 37. Migraine
*(Asked in: 2024-PI Part B Q7)*
- **Treatment:** Acute — NSAIDs (mild), Triptans (Sumatriptan, moderate-severe, contraindicated in IHD); Prophylaxis — Beta-blockers (Propranolol), Topiramate, Amitriptyline.
- **Key mediator in pathogenesis: Serotonin.**

### 38. Crohn's Disease vs Ulcerative Colitis
*(Asked in: 2024-PI Part B Q9, ER20-24T Part C Q20)*
- **Crohn's:** Transmural, skip lesions, anywhere mouth-to-anus, fistulas/strictures common.
- **UC:** Mucosal only, continuous, confined to colon/rectum.
- **First-line treatment for mild-moderate UC: Aminosalicylates** (e.g., Mesalamine).

### 39. Pneumonia
*(Asked in: ER20-24T Part B Q9, BTEUP Part C Q13 — fungal pneumonia)*
- **Pharmacological management:** Outpatient CAP — Amoxicillin/Azithromycin; Inpatient — Ceftriaxone + Macrolide.
- Fungal pneumonia is generally **non-contagious** (person-to-person).
