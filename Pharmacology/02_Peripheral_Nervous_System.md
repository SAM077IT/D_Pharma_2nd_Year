## Cholinergic drugs
Direct muscarinic agonists: pilocarpine, bethanechol.
Anticholinesterases: neostigmine, physostigmine.

**Pilocarpine:** muscarinic agonist; causes miosis and increased secretions; used in glaucoma/dry mouth in selected cases.

**Atropine:** antimuscarinic; reduces secretions, increases heart rate, causes mydriasis, reduces GI motility. Uses include bradycardia and organophosphate poisoning with appropriate management.

## Local anaesthetics 🔴
Block voltage-gated Na⁺ channels → prevent nerve impulse conduction. Examples: lidocaine, bupivacaine, procaine. Toxicity can involve CNS and cardiovascular systems.

## NSAIDs 🔴
COX inhibition → reduced prostaglandin synthesis. Examples: aspirin, ibuprofen, diclofenac, indomethacin, naproxen.
Adverse effects: GI irritation/ulcer/bleeding, renal effects, fluid retention, hypersensitivity.

## Opioids
**Morphine:** analgesia, sedation, respiratory depression, reduced GI motility, miosis. Major adverse effect: respiratory depression.

## Neuromuscular blockers
Non-depolarizing: atracurium, vecuronium. Depolarizing: succinylcholine. Used for muscle relaxation during anaesthesia/intubation.
