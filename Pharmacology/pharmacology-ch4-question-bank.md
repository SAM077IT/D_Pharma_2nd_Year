## Section A — Long/Essay Questions (5 marks each)

**Q1. 📌 PYQ (2023).** What are General anaesthetics? Write the advantages and disadvantages of nitrous oxide.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
General anaesthetics are drugs that produce reversible loss of all sensation and consciousness, allowing painless surgery. Classified as inhalational (Nitrous oxide, Halothane, Isoflurane) and intravenous (Thiopentone, Propofol, Ketamine). **Nitrous oxide advantages:** rapid onset/recovery, good analgesia, non-irritant, non-flammable clinically, minimal cardiorespiratory depression. **Disadvantages:** weak potency (cannot produce surgical anaesthesia alone), risk of diffusion hypoxia on stopping (give 100% O2 after), expands closed air spaces (avoid in pneumothorax/bowel obstruction), prolonged use linked to bone marrow suppression/B12 inactivation.
</details>

**Q2. 📌 PYQ (2024).** Write in detail about the pharmacology of anti-convulsant drugs.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
Anti-convulsants prevent/control seizures by reducing abnormal neuronal excitability. **Classification by mechanism:** Sodium channel blockers (Phenytoin, Carbamazepine), GABA enhancers (Phenobarbitone, Benzodiazepines), Calcium channel modulators (Ethosuximide — T-type, absence seizures), multi-mechanism (Sodium valproate). Phenytoin blocks voltage-gated sodium channels, stabilizing neuronal membranes. Used for generalized tonic-clonic seizures (Phenytoin, Valproate), absence seizures (Ethosuximide), focal seizures (Carbamazepine), and status epilepticus (IV Diazepam then Phenytoin loading). Adverse effects: Phenytoin — gingival hyperplasia, ataxia; Valproate — hepatotoxicity, teratogenicity; Carbamazepine — aplastic anaemia (rare).
</details>

**Q3. 📌 PYQ (VMRF 2019).** Define and classify antidepressants. Describe the action of tricyclic antidepressants.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
Antidepressants treat depression by increasing monoamine (serotonin, norepinephrine) availability in the CNS. **Classification:** TCAs (Imipramine, Amitriptyline), SSRIs (Fluoxetine, Sertraline), SNRIs (Venlafaxine), MAOIs (Phenelzine), Atypical agents (Mirtazapine, Bupropion). **TCAs:** Block reuptake of both serotonin and norepinephrine at the presynaptic neuron, increasing synaptic levels; also block muscarinic, histaminic, and alpha-adrenergic receptors, explaining side effects (dry mouth, sedation, postural hypotension). Cardiotoxic in overdose — a major safety concern, now generally superseded by SSRIs as first-line.
</details>

**Q4.** Classify anti-psychotic drugs and describe their mechanism of action, indications, and adverse effects.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**Classification:** Typical/1st-generation (Chlorpromazine, Haloperidol — strong D2 blockade) and Atypical/2nd-generation (Risperidone, Olanzapine, Clozapine — D2 + 5-HT2A blockade). Primarily block dopamine D2 receptors in the mesolimbic pathway, reducing positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions); atypical agents' added serotonin blockade reduces EPS risk and helps negative symptoms. Indications: schizophrenia, acute mania, severe agitation. Adverse effects: EPS (dystonia, akathisia, Parkinsonism, tardive dyskinesia — more with typical agents), neuroleptic malignant syndrome (rare, life-threatening), Clozapine — agranulocytosis (requires blood monitoring), metabolic syndrome (more with atypical agents).
</details>

**Q5.** Discuss opioid analgesics — classification, mechanism of action, indications, and contraindications.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
Opioids relieve pain by acting on opioid receptors (mainly μ) in the CNS, mimicking endogenous opioids. **Classification:** Natural (Morphine, Codeine), Semi-synthetic (Oxycodone), Synthetic (Pethidine, Fentanyl, Tramadol), Antagonists (Naloxone), Mixed agonist-antagonists (Pentazocine, Buprenorphine). Actions: strong analgesia, sedation, respiratory depression, miosis, constipation, cough suppression. Indications: severe acute pain, chronic cancer pain (WHO ladder), pulmonary oedema (Morphine), cough (Codeine). Contraindications: respiratory failure, head injury (masks neurological signs, raises ICP), acute asthma; Naloxone is the specific antidote for overdose.
</details>

**Q6.** Classify hypnotics and sedatives with their mechanism of action and precautions.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**Classification:** Benzodiazepines (Diazepam, Nitrazepam), Barbiturates (Phenobarbitone), Z-drugs (Zolpidem, Zopiclone), Others (Chloral hydrate, Ramelteon). Benzodiazepines/Z-drugs enhance GABA-A receptor-mediated chloride channel opening, potentiating inhibitory neurotransmission; Barbiturates act similarly but with a narrower safety margin. Used for short-term insomnia, preoperative sedation, anxiety, alcohol withdrawal, status epilepticus (Diazepam). Precautions: respiratory depression risk, tolerance/dependence with prolonged use, avoid abrupt withdrawal (seizure risk), avoid with alcohol.
</details>

## Section B — Short Notes (3 marks each)

**Q7. 📌 PYQ (2024).** Give a brief note on General Anaesthetics.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Produce reversible loss of sensation/consciousness for surgery. Inhalational (N2O, Halothane, Isoflurane) or IV (Thiopentone, Propofol, Ketamine). Act mainly via GABA-A potentiation (or NMDA antagonism — Ketamine). Anaesthesia progresses through 4 stages (Guedel's); Stage III (surgical anaesthesia) is where procedures are performed.</details>

**Q8. 📌 PYQ (UPES).** What are extrapyramidal side effects? Name the drugs causing them.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Movement disorders from excess D2 blockade in the nigrostriatal pathway — dystonia, akathisia, drug-induced Parkinsonism, tardive dyskinesia (irreversible, long-term). Most associated with typical antipsychotics — Haloperidol, Chlorpromazine, Trifluoperazine.</details>

**Q9. 📌 PYQ (UPES).** Discuss the mechanism of antidepressant drugs.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Most classes increase synaptic availability of serotonin and/or norepinephrine. TCAs and SNRIs block reuptake of both; SSRIs selectively block serotonin reuptake; MAOIs inhibit the enzyme that breaks down monoamines. Clinical benefit is delayed 2–4 weeks despite the immediate rise in neurotransmitter levels, due to downstream receptor adaptation.</details>

**Q10. 📌 PYQ (UPES).** Classify antiepileptic drugs.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>By mechanism: Sodium channel blockers (Phenytoin, Carbamazepine), GABA enhancers (Phenobarbitone, Benzodiazepines), Calcium channel modulators (Ethosuximide), multi-mechanism agents (Sodium valproate), newer agents (Levetiracetam, Gabapentin, Topiramate).</details>

**Q11.** Write a short note on Phenytoin.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Sodium channel blocker anti-convulsant; stabilizes neuronal membranes, preventing repetitive firing. Used for generalized tonic-clonic and focal seizures. Narrow therapeutic index (10–20 mcg/mL). ADRs: gingival hyperplasia, hirsutism, ataxia/nystagmus at toxic levels, teratogenic.</details>

**Q12.** Write a short note on Ketamine.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>IV general anaesthetic; NMDA receptor antagonist (dissociative anaesthesia). Maintains airway reflexes and blood pressure, useful in emergency/field settings and paediatrics. Causes emergence delirium/hallucinations as a notable side effect.</details>

**Q13.** Write a short note on Clozapine.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Atypical antipsychotic, reserved for treatment-resistant schizophrenia. Blocks D2 and 5-HT2A receptors with low EPS risk. Requires mandatory regular blood count monitoring due to agranulocytosis risk.</details>

**Q14.** Write a short note on Baclofen.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Centrally acting muscle relaxant; GABA-B receptor agonist, inhibits excitatory neurotransmitter release in the spinal cord. Used for spasticity (spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis). Avoid abrupt withdrawal — risk of seizures/hallucinations.</details>

**Q15.** Write a short note on Naloxone.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Competitive opioid receptor antagonist; rapidly reverses opioid-induced respiratory depression/overdose by displacing opioids from receptors. Shorter duration of action than most opioids — repeat dosing/monitoring often needed.</details>

**Q16.** Write a short note on Buspirone.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Anxiolytic; partial agonist at serotonin 5-HT1A receptors, distinct from the GABA mechanism of benzodiazepines. Non-sedating, no dependence risk, but slow onset (2–4 weeks) — unsuitable for acute anxiety/panic attacks.</details>

**Q17.** Write a short note on Sodium valproate.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Anti-convulsant with dual mechanism — increases GABA levels and blocks sodium channels. Broad-spectrum (generalized and absence seizures). Key risks: hepatotoxicity and strong teratogenicity (neural tube defects) — avoided in pregnancy.</details>

**Q18.** Write a short note on Piracetam.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Prototype nootropic agent; thought to enhance neuronal membrane fluidity and cerebral blood flow (exact mechanism unclear). Used adjunctively in post-stroke rehabilitation and myoclonus. Generally well-tolerated; caution in renal impairment.</details>

**Q19.** Write a short note on Codeine.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Natural opioid, weaker analgesic potency than Morphine. Commonly used as an antitussive (cough suppressant) at low doses and mild-moderate analgesic. Shares opioid ADRs (constipation, respiratory depression at high dose).</details>

## Section C — MCQ / Fill in the Blanks / One-word (1 mark each)

**Q20. 📌 PYQ (2023).** Stages of Anaesthesia are divided into ______________.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Four (stages)</details>

**Q21. 📌 PYQ (2024).** Enlist the stages of anaesthesia.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Stage I – Analgesia; Stage II – Excitement/Delirium; Stage III – Surgical anaesthesia; Stage IV – Medullary paralysis.</details>

**Q22. 📌 PYQ (2023).** Which drug is used as a centrally acting muscle relaxant?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Diazepam</details>

**Q23. 📌 PYQ (2023).** Diphenylhydantoin is used in the treatment of _____________.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Epilepsy</details>

**Q24. 📌 PYQ (2023).** Morphine is a ____________ analgesic.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Narcotic (opioid)</details>

**Q25.** Which stage of anaesthesia is considered the most dangerous, and why?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Stage II (Excitement/Delirium) — reflexes still active but consciousness impaired, risk of laryngospasm and irregular cardiorespiratory responses.</details>

**Q26.** Name the specific antidote used to reverse benzodiazepine overdose.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Flumazenil</details>

**Q27.** Which anti-convulsant is specifically used for absence seizures via T-type calcium channel blockade?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ethosuximide</details>

**Q28.** Name the class of drugs that must have dietary tyramine restriction.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>MAOIs (Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors)</details>

**Q29.** Which antipsychotic requires mandatory regular blood count monitoring, and for what risk?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Clozapine — risk of agranulocytosis.</details>

**Q30.** Name the receptor primarily responsible for opioid analgesic action.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Mu (μ) opioid receptor</details>

**Q31.** What is the classic pupillary sign of opioid overdose?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Miosis (pinpoint pupils)</details>

**Q32.** Name a nootropic drug specifically used in Alzheimer's disease.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Donepezil (acetylcholinesterase inhibitor)</details>

**Q33.** Which general anaesthetic is an NMDA receptor antagonist rather than a GABA potentiator?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ketamine</details>

**Q34.** Name the withdrawal risk associated with abrupt discontinuation of long-term benzodiazepine use.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Withdrawal seizures</details>

**Q35.** Which class of antidepressants is generally considered first-line today due to a better side-effect profile than TCAs?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>SSRIs (Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors)</details>
