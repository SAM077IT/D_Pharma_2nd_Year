## ADME
**A:** absorption into systemic circulation. **D:** distribution to tissues. **M:** biotransformation. **E:** removal of drug/metabolites.

### Bioavailability 🔴
Fraction of administered dose reaching systemic circulation unchanged. IV administration is generally 100%. Oral availability may fall because of incomplete absorption and first-pass metabolism.

**Factors:** route, formulation, food, GI motility/pH, absorption, first-pass metabolism, drug interactions, disease/patient factors.

### Routes
Enteral: oral, sublingual, rectal. Parenteral: IV, IM, SC.

**Parenteral advantages:** rapid effect, useful when oral route unsuitable, useful in unconscious/vomiting patients, precise delivery.
**Disadvantages:** pain, infection, skill required, irreversible after injection, cost, tissue injury.

### Biotransformation
**Phase I:** oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis.
**Phase II:** conjugation such as glucuronidation, sulfation, acetylation.

### Excretion
Kidney is major; also bile/faeces, lungs, sweat, saliva and breast milk.
