## Section A — Long/Essay Questions (5 marks each)

**Q1. 📌 PYQ (2023).** Explain briefly the pharmacology of cholinergic and anticholinergic drugs.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**Cholinergic drugs** mimic acetylcholine at muscarinic/nicotinic receptors — classified as direct-acting (Pilocarpine, Carbachol) or indirect-acting/anticholinesterases (Neostigmine, Physostigmine). They cause SLUDGE effects (salivation, lacrimation, urination, defecation, GI upset, emesis), bradycardia, miosis, bronchoconstriction. Used in glaucoma (Pilocarpine) and myasthenia gravis (Neostigmine). **Anticholinergic drugs** (e.g., Atropine, Hyoscine) block muscarinic receptors, causing mydriasis, tachycardia, reduced secretions, bronchodilation. Used as an antidote for organophosphate poisoning, preanaesthetic medication, and for pupil dilation. Contraindicated in glaucoma (can worsen angle closure) and prostatic hypertrophy.
</details>

**Q2. 📌 PYQ (2024).** What are NSAIDs? Classify them and give their indications and contraindications.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
NSAIDs (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs) have analgesic, antipyretic, and anti-inflammatory actions by inhibiting cyclooxygenase (COX) enzymes, reducing prostaglandin synthesis. **Classification:** Salicylates (Aspirin), Propionic acid derivatives (Ibuprofen, Naproxen), Acetic acid derivatives (Diclofenac, Indomethacin), Fenamates (Mefenamic acid), Oxicams (Piroxicam), Selective COX-2 inhibitors (Celecoxib). **Indications:** mild-moderate pain, fever, inflammatory conditions (RA, OA, gout), low-dose Aspirin for antiplatelet effect. **Contraindications:** peptic ulcer/GI bleeding history, renal impairment, aspirin-sensitive asthma, third-trimester pregnancy, bleeding disorders, children with viral infection (Aspirin — Reye's syndrome).
</details>

**Q3.** Classify neuromuscular blocking agents and describe their mechanism of action, uses, and precautions.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**Classification:** Non-depolarizing/competitive (D-Tubocurarine, Vecuronium, Atracurium, Rocuronium) and Depolarizing (Succinylcholine). Non-depolarizing agents competitively block nicotinic (N2) receptors at the motor end-plate — reversible with anticholinesterases (Neostigmine). Succinylcholine causes persistent depolarization leading to flaccid paralysis — not reversed by anticholinesterases. Used as an adjunct to general anaesthesia for muscle relaxation during surgery and to facilitate intubation. Precautions: must have ventilatory support available (diaphragm paralysis affects breathing); Succinylcholine contraindicated in hyperkalaemia, burns, and malignant hyperthermia-susceptible patients; caution in myasthenia gravis (exaggerated response).
</details>

**Q4. 📌 PYQ-style (based on VMRF 2019: "Write a note on neuromuscular blocking agent" — expanded).** Describe the drugs used in the management of myasthenia gravis.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
Myasthenia gravis is an autoimmune disease with antibodies against nicotinic ACh receptors at the neuromuscular junction, causing fluctuating muscle weakness. **Mainstay treatment:** anticholinesterases (Pyridostigmine, Neostigmine) — inhibit acetylcholinesterase, increasing ACh availability at remaining receptors. **Diagnostic agent:** Edrophonium (short-acting anticholinesterase — Tensilon test, transiently improves strength). **Immunosuppressants** (Corticosteroids, Azathioprine) address the underlying autoimmune process. **Severe/refractory cases:** plasmapheresis, IV immunoglobulin, thymectomy. Caution: anticholinesterase overdose can cause a cholinergic crisis, which paradoxically also causes weakness — distinguished from myasthenic crisis by the Edrophonium test response.
</details>

**Q5.** Classify local anaesthetic agents and explain their mechanism of action with clinical uses.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**Classification:** Esters (Procaine, Tetracaine — hydrolyzed by plasma pseudocholinesterase, higher allergy risk) and Amides (Lidocaine, Bupivacaine — hepatic metabolism, lower allergy risk, more widely used). **Mechanism:** Block voltage-gated sodium channels in the nerve membrane, preventing action potential generation and conduction — reversible block, pain fibres affected first. **Uses:** infiltration anaesthesia, nerve blocks, spinal/epidural anaesthesia, topical anaesthesia; Lidocaine also used IV as an anti-arrhythmic. Adrenaline is often co-administered to prolong action via vasoconstriction, but avoided in end-arterial sites (fingers, toes, nose) due to ischaemia risk.
</details>

**Q6.** Classify adrenergic (sympathomimetic) drugs by receptor selectivity, with examples and one clinical use each.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**Alpha-1 agonists** — Phenylephrine (nasal decongestion, vasoconstriction). **Alpha-2 agonists** — Clonidine (hypertension, via central sympathetic inhibition). **Beta-1 agonists** — Dobutamine (cardiogenic shock). **Beta-2 agonists** — Salbutamol (asthma/bronchospasm). **Non-selective/mixed** — Adrenaline (α, β1, β2 — anaphylaxis, cardiac arrest), Noradrenaline (α, β1 — septic shock), Dopamine (dose-dependent receptor activity — shock states), Isoprenaline (β1, β2 — heart block, historically).
</details>

**Q7. 📌 PYQ-style (based on UPES: "Explain the pharmacology of adrenaline" — and VMRF: "Write short notes on Sympathetic blocking agents" — combined).** Classify anti-adrenergic (sympatholytic) drugs and describe their clinical uses and contraindications.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**Classification:** Alpha-blockers — non-selective (Phenoxybenzamine, Phentolamine) and selective α1-blockers (Prazosin, Tamsulosin); Beta-blockers — non-selective (Propranolol) and cardioselective β1-blockers (Atenolol, Metoprolol); Mixed α/β blockers (Labetalol, Carvedilol); Indirect sympathetic blockers (Reserpine, Guanethidine). **Uses:** hypertension, angina, arrhythmias, post-MI (beta-blockers), phaeochromocytoma preoperative management (Phenoxybenzamine), BPH (Tamsulosin), migraine prophylaxis, essential tremor (Propranolol). **Contraindications:** Beta-blockers avoided in asthma/COPD (bronchoconstriction) and severe bradycardia/heart block; alpha-blockers can cause first-dose postural hypotension.
</details>

## Section B — Short Notes (3 marks each)

**Q8. 📌 PYQ (2024).** Write a short note on anti-cholinergic drugs.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Block muscarinic receptors, opposing parasympathetic effects. Examples: Atropine, Hyoscine, Ipratropium. Actions: mydriasis, tachycardia, reduced secretions, bronchodilation. Uses: OP poisoning antidote, preanaesthetic medication, mydriasis for eye exam. Contraindicated in glaucoma, prostatic hypertrophy.</details>

**Q9. 📌 PYQ (2024).** Give a brief account of pharmacology of beta blockers.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Competitively block β-adrenergic receptors. Non-selective (Propranolol) block β1 & β2; cardioselective (Atenolol, Metoprolol) mainly block β1. Reduce heart rate, contractility, BP, renin release. Used in hypertension, angina, arrhythmias, post-MI, migraine prophylaxis. Avoided in asthma/COPD (non-selective agents) and severe bradycardia/heart block.</details>

**Q10. 📌 PYQ (2023).** Write notes on Non-Steroidal Anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Inhibit COX-1/COX-2, reducing prostaglandin synthesis — analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory. Examples: Aspirin, Ibuprofen, Diclofenac, Celecoxib (COX-2 selective). Used for pain, fever, inflammatory arthritis. ADRs: GI bleeding/ulceration, nephrotoxicity, aspirin-induced Reye's syndrome in children.</details>

**Q11. 📌 PYQ (2023).** Write notes on drugs used in glaucoma.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Pilocarpine (cholinergic agonist) — increases aqueous outflow via ciliary muscle contraction. Timolol (topical beta-blocker) — reduces aqueous humour production. Acetazolamide (carbonic anhydrase inhibitor) — reduces aqueous production. Latanoprost (prostaglandin analogue) — increases uveoscleral outflow, first-line in chronic open-angle glaucoma.</details>

**Q12. 📌 PYQ (VMRF 2019).** Write a short note on D-Tubocurarine.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Prototype non-depolarizing neuromuscular blocker. Competitively blocks nicotinic (N2) receptors at the motor end-plate, preventing depolarization by ACh — flaccid paralysis. Reversed by anticholinesterases (Neostigmine). Can cause histamine release, leading to hypotension and bronchospasm.</details>

**Q13. 📌 PYQ (VMRF 2019).** Write a short note on Physostigmine.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Reversible anticholinesterase, a naturally-occurring alkaloid; crosses the blood-brain barrier (unlike Neostigmine). Used in the treatment of anticholinergic drug overdose/central anticholinergic syndrome and in glaucoma (topically, historically). Excess causes cholinergic crisis (SLUDGE symptoms).</details>

**Q14. 📌 PYQ (UPES).** Write a short note on myasthenia gravis (drug treatment).
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Autoimmune destruction of nicotinic ACh receptors at the neuromuscular junction causing fluctuating muscle weakness. First-line treatment: Pyridostigmine (anticholinesterase) to increase ACh availability. Severe cases: corticosteroids, azathioprine, plasmapheresis, IVIG, thymectomy. Diagnosis via Edrophonium (Tensilon) test.</details>

**Q15.** Write a short note on Succinylcholine.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Depolarizing neuromuscular blocker; causes persistent depolarization of the motor end-plate — initial fasciculations followed by flaccid paralysis. Rapid onset, short duration (used for quick intubation). Not reversed by anticholinesterases. Contraindicated in hyperkalaemia, burns, and malignant hyperthermia-susceptible patients.</details>

**Q16.** Write a short note on Lidocaine.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Amide-type local anaesthetic; blocks voltage-gated sodium channels, preventing nerve impulse conduction. Used for infiltration/nerve block anaesthesia and IV as a Class Ib anti-arrhythmic (ventricular arrhythmias). Often combined with Adrenaline to prolong local action (avoided in end-arterial sites).</details>

**Q17.** Write a short note on Aspirin.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Salicylate NSAID; irreversibly inhibits COX-1 and COX-2. Used as analgesic/antipyretic (higher dose) and antiplatelet agent (low dose, 75–150 mg/day — irreversible platelet COX-1 inhibition lasts the platelet's lifespan). Avoided in children with viral fever (Reye's syndrome) and in peptic ulcer disease.</details>

**Q18.** Write a short note on Prazosin.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Selective α1-adrenergic blocker. Causes vasodilation (↓ BP) and reduces resistance to urinary outflow. Used in hypertension and benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH). Main caution: first-dose postural hypotension — first dose given at bedtime.</details>

**Q19.** Write a short note on Edrophonium.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Very short-acting, reversible anticholinesterase. Used diagnostically in the Edrophonium (Tensilon) test to distinguish myasthenic crisis (strength improves) from cholinergic crisis (weakness worsens) in a patient with myasthenia gravis.</details>

**Q20.** Write a short note on the steps of neurohumoral transmission.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Five sequential steps: (1) Synthesis of transmitter, (2) Storage in vesicles, (3) Release into synaptic cleft (calcium-dependent), (4) Binding to postsynaptic receptors, (5) Termination of action via enzymatic degradation (e.g., acetylcholinesterase) or reuptake.</details>

## Section C — MCQ / Fill in the Blanks / One-word (1 mark each)

**Q21. 📌 PYQ (2023).** Atropine sulphate is used as __________.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>a) Mydriatic drug</details>

**Q22. 📌 PYQ (2024).** Define Mydriatics.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Drugs that dilate the pupil by blocking the sphincter pupillae's muscarinic receptors (e.g., Atropine, Tropicamide).</details>

**Q23. 📌 PYQ (2024).** Name any two drugs belonging to anti-adrenergic drugs.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Any two of: Propranolol, Atenolol, Prazosin, Phenoxybenzamine, Labetalol.</details>

**Q24. 📌 PYQ (2024).** Give a name of drug used in Glaucoma.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Any one of: Pilocarpine, Timolol, Latanoprost, Acetazolamide.</details>

**Q25. 📌 PYQ (2024).** Which of the following also acts as a local anaesthetic — Quinidine / Procainamide / Lidocaine / Esmolol?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Lidocaine</details>

**Q26.** What is the drug of choice for anaphylactic shock?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Adrenaline (Epinephrine)</details>

**Q27.** Name the depolarizing neuromuscular blocking agent.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Succinylcholine (Suxamethonium)</details>

**Q28.** Which enzyme do anticholinesterase drugs inhibit?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Acetylcholinesterase</details>

**Q29.** Name the test used to diagnose myasthenia gravis.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Edrophonium (Tensilon) test</details>

**Q30.** Which local anaesthetic class is metabolized by plasma pseudocholinesterase — esters or amides?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Esters</details>

**Q31.** Name the COX isoform mainly responsible for protective gastric mucosal prostaglandins.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>COX-1</details>

**Q32.** Which NSAID is specifically associated with Reye's syndrome in children?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Aspirin</details>

**Q33.** Name the antidote used in organophosphate poisoning that regenerates acetylcholinesterase.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Pralidoxime (along with Atropine for symptomatic muscarinic blockade)</details>

**Q34.** Which neurotransmitter is released by all preganglionic autonomic fibres?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Acetylcholine</details>

**Q35.** Name a selective β2 agonist used in asthma.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Salbutamol (or Terbutaline)</details>

**Q36.** Should adrenaline be added to local anaesthetic for a finger block? Why/why not?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>No — fingers are end-arterial sites; adrenaline-induced vasoconstriction risks ischaemia/necrosis.</details>
