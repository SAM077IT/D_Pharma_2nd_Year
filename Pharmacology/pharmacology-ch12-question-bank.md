## Section A — Long/Essay Questions (5 marks each — practice writing these in ~7–8 min each)

**Q1. 📌 PYQ (2023).** Give the basic principle of chemotherapy of infections.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
Chemotherapy is the use of chemical agents to destroy an infecting organism without significantly harming the host — based on the principle of **selective toxicity** (exploiting biochemical differences between pathogen and host, e.g., bacterial cell wall synthesis targeted by penicillins). Basic principles include: (1) identify the causative organism via culture/sensitivity where possible; (2) choose the narrowest effective spectrum to limit resistance/superinfection; (3) consider host factors — age, renal/hepatic function, pregnancy, allergy; (4) ensure the drug reaches adequate concentration at the site of infection; (5) give adequate dose, correct route, and full duration — incomplete courses promote resistance; (6) consider cost/availability. Drugs may be **bacteriostatic** (inhibit growth) or **bactericidal** (kill directly).
</details>

**Q2. 📌 PYQ-style (based on 2024: "Give a short note on Penicillins" — expanded to essay level).** Classify Penicillins and describe their mechanism of action, dose, indications and contraindications.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**Classification:** Natural (Benzylpenicillin, Phenoxymethylpenicillin); Penicillinase-resistant (Methicillin, Cloxacillin); Extended-spectrum/Aminopenicillins (Ampicillin, Amoxicillin); Antipseudomonal (Piperacillin). **Mechanism:** Inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins, blocking peptidoglycan cross-linking → cell lysis (bactericidal). **Dose:** Benzylpenicillin 0.6–1.2 g IM/IV 6-hourly; Amoxicillin 250–500 mg orally 8-hourly. **Indications:** Streptococcal infections, pneumococcal pneumonia, syphilis, meningococcal meningitis. **Contraindications:** Known penicillin hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis risk); caution in renal impairment.
</details>

**Q3. 📌 PYQ (2024).** Write a descriptive note on anti-malarial agents.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
Anti-malarials are classified by the parasite stage they act on: **tissue schizonticides** (Primaquine — liver stage), **blood schizonticides** (Chloroquine, Artesunate, Quinine — erythrocytic stage, cure acute attack), **hypnozoiticides** (Primaquine — radical cure of dormant *P. vivax/ovale* liver hypnozoites), and **gametocytocides** (Primaquine, Artemisinin derivatives — prevent transmission). Chloroquine inhibits haem polymerase causing toxic haem buildup; Artemisinins generate free radicals via endoperoxide cleavage (fastest-acting). Dose example: Chloroquine 25 mg/kg total over 3 days for *P. vivax*; Artemisinin Combination Therapy for *P. falciparum*. Indications: treatment and chemoprophylaxis of malaria. Contraindications: check G6PD status before Primaquine (haemolysis risk); Chloroquine — retinal toxicity with prolonged use; Quinine — cinchonism, hypoglycaemia.
</details>

**Q4.** Classify anti-tubercular drugs. Describe the mechanism of action and adverse effects of first-line drugs.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**First-line:** Rifampicin, Isoniazid, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol, Streptomycin. **Second-line:** Fluoroquinolones, Kanamycin/Amikacin, Ethionamide, Cycloserine, PAS. Mechanisms: Rifampicin — inhibits RNA polymerase; Isoniazid — inhibits mycolic acid synthesis; Pyrazinamide — active in acidic macrophage pH; Ethambutol — inhibits arabinosyl transferase; Streptomycin — inhibits 30S ribosomal protein synthesis. Adverse effects: Rifampicin — hepatotoxicity, orange secretions, enzyme induction; Isoniazid — peripheral neuropathy (prevented by Pyridoxine), hepatotoxicity; Pyrazinamide — hepatotoxicity, hyperuricaemia; Ethambutol — optic neuritis; Streptomycin — oto/nephrotoxicity. Drugs are always combined (never monotherapy) to prevent resistance.
</details>

**Q5. 📌 PYQ-style (based on 2023: "Classify anti-viral drugs" — expanded to essay level).** Classify anti-viral drugs with examples and describe the mechanism of Acyclovir.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**Classification:** Anti-herpes (Acyclovir, Valacyclovir); Anti-influenza (Oseltamivir, Zanamivir); Anti-retroviral/HIV (Zidovudine, Nevirapine, Ritonavir, Dolutegravir); Anti-hepatitis (Tenofovir, Sofosbuvir); Broad-spectrum (Ribavirin). **Acyclovir mechanism:** A guanosine analogue activated by viral thymidine kinase (present in herpes-infected cells but not efficiently in normal cells), then incorporated into viral DNA, terminating chain synthesis — giving high selective toxicity for infected cells only. Used for genital herpes, herpes zoster, chickenpox. Dose adjustment needed in renal impairment; ensure hydration to avoid crystalluria.
</details>

**Q6.** Define anti-neoplastic drugs and classify them according to their mechanism of action.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
Anti-neoplastic (anticancer) drugs are chemical agents used to destroy or inhibit the growth of malignant (cancer) cells, generally by targeting rapidly dividing cells. **Classification by mechanism:** (1) Alkylating agents — Cyclophosphamide, Chlorambucil (cross-link DNA); (2) Antimetabolites — Methotrexate, 5-Fluorouracil (block DNA/RNA synthesis as structural analogues); (3) Antitumour antibiotics — Doxorubicin, Bleomycin (intercalate DNA/cause strand breaks); (4) Plant alkaloids — Vincristine, Paclitaxel (disrupt microtubules, arrest mitosis); (5) Hormonal agents — Tamoxifen, Prednisolone; (6) Topoisomerase inhibitors — Etoposide; (7) Targeted/biologic agents — Imatinib, Trastuzumab. Because rapidly dividing normal cells (bone marrow, GI mucosa, hair follicles) are also affected, common toxicities include myelosuppression, mucositis, and alopecia.
</details>

**Q7.** Describe the classification, mechanism, dose, indications and contraindications of Aminoglycosides.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**Examples:** Streptomycin, Gentamicin, Amikacin, Tobramycin, Neomycin. **Mechanism:** Irreversibly bind the 30S ribosomal subunit, causing misreading of mRNA and inhibiting protein synthesis — bactericidal, mainly against aerobic Gram-negative bacilli. **Dose:** Gentamicin 3–5 mg/kg/day IV/IM in divided doses. **Indications:** Severe Gram-negative infections (septicaemia, complicated UTI), often with a beta-lactam for synergy; Streptomycin in anti-TB regimens. **Contraindications:** Ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity (dose-limiting) — avoid in renal impairment and pregnancy where possible; caution in myasthenia gravis (neuromuscular blockade).
</details>

**Q8.** Discuss the classification, mechanism of action and adverse effects of anti-fungal drugs.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**Classification:** Polyenes (Amphotericin B, Nystatin); Azoles (Fluconazole, Ketoconazole, Itraconazole); Allylamines (Terbinafine); Echinocandins (Caspofungin); Antimetabolite (Flucytosine). **Mechanism:** Polyenes bind ergosterol in the fungal membrane causing pore formation (fungicidal); Azoles inhibit ergosterol synthesis via CYP-dependent 14-α-demethylase (fungistatic); Echinocandins inhibit fungal cell wall β-glucan synthesis. **Adverse effects:** Amphotericin B — significant nephrotoxicity, infusion-related fever/chills, hypokalaemia; Azoles — hepatotoxicity, multiple CYP450 drug interactions. Amphotericin B is toxic partly because fungal ergosterol and human cholesterol are structurally similar, so selective toxicity is imperfect.
</details>

**Q9.** Classify anti-amoebic agents based on their site of action and explain the use of Metronidazole.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**Classification:** Tissue amoebicides (Metronidazole, Tinidazole — act on amoebae in intestinal wall/liver); Luminal amoebicides (Diloxanide furoate, Paromomycin — act only in the gut lumen); Mixed amoebicides (Chloroquine, adjunct for hepatic amoebiasis). **Metronidazole:** Undergoes reduction inside the anaerobic organism, forming cytotoxic free radicals that damage microbial DNA — active only under anaerobic conditions. Used for invasive intestinal amoebiasis and amoebic liver abscess, dose 400–800 mg orally three times daily for 5–10 days. Must be followed by a luminal amoebicide to clear residual cysts and prevent relapse. Avoid alcohol (disulfiram-like reaction).
</details>

**Q10.** Classify anthelmintic drugs according to the type of worm infestation they treat, with mechanism of action.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**By worm type:** Roundworms/hookworms/pinworms — Albendazole, Mebendazole, Pyrantel pamoate; Filariasis — Diethylcarbamazine (DEC, drug of choice); Tapeworms (cestodes) — Praziquantel, Niclosamide; Schistosomiasis (flukes) — Praziquantel; Strongyloidiasis/onchocerciasis — Ivermectin. **Mechanism:** Albendazole/Mebendazole inhibit microtubule (tubulin) polymerization, impairing glucose uptake by the worm; Pyrantel causes depolarizing neuromuscular (spastic) paralysis; DEC immobilizes microfilariae, exposing them to host immune destruction; Praziquantel increases calcium permeability, causing paralysis. Albendazole and Mebendazole should be avoided in the first trimester of pregnancy.
</details>

**Q11.** Describe the classification of Cephalosporins by generation with examples and clinical uses.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**1st generation:** Cefazolin, Cephalexin — good Gram-positive cover, surgical prophylaxis. **2nd generation:** Cefuroxime, Cefaclor — extended Gram-negative cover. **3rd generation:** Ceftriaxone, Cefotaxime, Cefixime — wide Gram-negative cover, good CNS penetration (Ceftriaxone used in meningitis, typhoid, gonorrhoea). **4th generation:** Cefepime — extended spectrum, active against Pseudomonas. **5th generation:** Ceftaroline — MRSA coverage. Mechanism: inhibit cell wall synthesis via PBP binding (bactericidal, beta-lactam ring). Adverse effects: hypersensitivity, partial cross-reactivity with penicillin allergy, dose adjustment needed in renal impairment.
</details>

**Q12.** Write in detail on Sulphonamides — classification, mechanism of action, dose, indications and contraindications.
<details>
<summary>Model Answer</summary>
**Examples:** Sulfadiazine, Sulfamethoxazole (combined with Trimethoprim as Co-trimoxazole), Sulfasalazine, Silver sulfadiazine (topical). Structurally similar to **PABA (para-aminobenzoic acid)**. **Mechanism:** Competitively inhibit dihydropteroate synthase in the bacterial folate synthesis pathway (bacteria must synthesize their own folate, unlike humans) — bacteriostatic. **Dose:** Co-trimoxazole 800/160 mg orally twice daily. **Indications:** UTI, Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (esp. in HIV), toxoplasmosis, burns (Silver sulfadiazine). **Contraindications:** Hypersensitivity (Stevens-Johnson syndrome), avoid near term in pregnancy/newborns (kernicterus risk), G6PD deficiency (haemolysis), ensure adequate hydration (crystalluria risk).
</details>

## Section B — Short Notes (3 marks each — aim for ~4–5 lines per answer)

**Q13. 📌 PYQ (2023).** Write notes on sulphonamides.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Structurally resemble PABA; competitively inhibit bacterial folate synthesis (dihydropteroate synthase) — bacteriostatic. Example: Co-trimoxazole. Used for UTI, PCP prophylaxis. Avoid near-term pregnancy (kernicterus), caution in G6PD deficiency.</details>

**Q14. 📌 PYQ (2024).** Give a short note on Penicillins.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Beta-lactam antibiotics inhibiting bacterial cell wall synthesis via PBP binding — bactericidal. Classified as natural, penicillinase-resistant, extended-spectrum. Used for streptococcal infections, syphilis. Main risk: hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis.</details>

**Q15.** Write a short note on Fluoroquinolones.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Inhibit bacterial DNA gyrase/topoisomerase IV — bactericidal, broad-spectrum. Examples: Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin. Used for UTI, typhoid, respiratory infections. Avoided in children/pregnancy (arthropathy risk); avoid with dairy/antacids (chelation).</details>

**Q16.** Write a short note on Macrolides.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Bind 50S ribosomal subunit, inhibit protein synthesis — mainly bacteriostatic. Examples: Erythromycin, Azithromycin. Used as penicillin-allergy alternative, atypical pneumonia. Caution: GI upset, QT prolongation, CYP3A4 interactions (Erythromycin).</details>

**Q17.** Write a short note on Tetracyclines.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Bind 30S ribosomal subunit, block aminoacyl-tRNA binding — bacteriostatic, broad-spectrum. Examples: Doxycycline, Tetracycline. Used for rickettsial/chlamydial infections, acne. Avoided in children <8 yrs and pregnancy (teeth/bone effects); avoid with milk/antacids.</details>

**Q18.** Write a short note on anti-tubercular drug-induced hepatotoxicity.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Rifampicin, Isoniazid, and Pyrazinamide are all individually hepatotoxic, and the risk is additive in combination therapy. Requires baseline and periodic liver function monitoring; therapy may need modification if significant transaminase elevation or jaundice occurs.</details>

**Q19.** Write a short note on Amphotericin B.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Polyene antifungal; binds ergosterol in fungal membrane causing pore formation — fungicidal. Drug of choice for many severe systemic mycoses despite toxicity. Major toxicities: nephrotoxicity, infusion-related fever/chills, hypokalaemia.</details>

**Q20. 📌 PYQ-style (short version of 2024's "antiviral" item).** Give two examples of anti-viral drugs with their use.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Acyclovir — herpes simplex/zoster infections (inhibits viral DNA synthesis after activation by viral thymidine kinase). Oseltamivir — influenza A & B (neuraminidase inhibitor, blocks release of new virions).</details>

**Q21. 📌 PYQ-style (short version of 2024's "Aminoglycosides" item).** Give examples of Aminoglycosides with one clinical use.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Gentamicin, Amikacin, Streptomycin, Tobramycin. Used for severe Gram-negative infections (e.g., septicaemia); Streptomycin specifically used as part of anti-TB combination therapy.</details>

**Q22.** Write a short note on Diethylcarbamazine (DEC).
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Drug of choice for lymphatic filariasis. Immobilizes microfilariae and alters their surface, exposing them to host immune destruction. Caution: can trigger a severe Mazzotti allergic reaction in onchocerciasis patients from rapid microfilarial death — cover with antihistamines/steroids.</details>

**Q23.** Write a short note on Primaquine.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>The only anti-malarial providing radical cure of *P. vivax/P. ovale* by eliminating dormant liver hypnozoites; also gametocytocidal. G6PD status must be checked before use — risk of severe haemolysis in deficient patients. Avoided in pregnancy.</details>

**Q24.** Write a short note on Metronidazole.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Nitroimidazole; reduced inside anaerobic organisms to cytotoxic free radicals that damage DNA. Used for invasive amoebiasis, giardiasis, anaerobic infections, trichomoniasis. Avoid alcohol during/after treatment (disulfiram-like reaction).</details>

**Q25.** Write a short note on drug resistance in chemotherapy.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Loss of susceptibility of a previously sensitive organism to a drug. Mechanisms: enzymatic drug inactivation (e.g., beta-lactamase), altered target site, decreased drug uptake, increased efflux, alternative metabolic pathways. Prevented by rational prescribing, completing full courses, and combination therapy (e.g., anti-TB regimens).</details>

**Q26.** Write a short note on superinfection.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>A new infection that appears during treatment of a primary infection, caused by disturbance of the normal protective microbial flora — common with broad-spectrum antibiotics (e.g., *C. difficile* colitis after clindamycin/broad-spectrum antibiotic use, or candidiasis).</details>

**Q27.** Write a short note on Vincristine and Vinblastine.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Plant (vinca) alkaloid anti-neoplastic agents; bind tubulin and disrupt microtubule formation, arresting mitosis at metaphase. Used in leukaemias, lymphomas, and various solid tumours (combination regimens). Vincristine — peripheral neuropathy is dose-limiting; Vinblastine — myelosuppression is more prominent.</details>

**Q28.** Write a short note on Ivermectin.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Anthelmintic active against Strongyloides and Onchocerca (river blindness); also used topically/orally for scabies. Enhances GABA-mediated neurotransmission in the parasite, causing paralysis.</details>

**Q29.** Write a short note on Chloroquine.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Blood schizonticide; concentrates in the parasite's food vacuole and inhibits haem polymerase, causing toxic haem accumulation that kills the parasite. Used for *P. vivax* malaria treatment. Caution: retinal toxicity with prolonged use.</details>

**Q30.** Write a short note on Griseofulvin.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>An older oral antifungal, deposits in keratin precursor cells, making new keratin resistant to fungal invasion — used for dermatophyte (ringworm) infections of skin/hair/nails, requires prolonged treatment as it doesn't kill existing fungus directly.</details>

**Q31.** Write a short note on Cephalosporin generations.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Classified 1st to 5th generation with increasing Gram-negative spectrum and decreasing Gram-positive potency (except 5th gen, which covers MRSA). 1st gen (Cefazolin) — surgical prophylaxis; 3rd gen (Ceftriaxone) — meningitis, typhoid; 4th gen (Cefepime) — Pseudomonas cover.</details>

**Q32.** Write a short note on Praziquantel.
<details><summary>Model Answer</summary>Anthelmintic effective against tapeworms (cestodes) and Schistosoma (flukes). Increases calcium permeability of the worm's cell membrane, causing paralysis and dislodgement from the host.</details>

## Section C — MCQ / Fill in the Blanks / One-word (1 mark each — rapid-fire revision)

**Q33. 📌 PYQ (2023).** Sulphonamide are structurally similar to __________.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA)</details>

**Q34. 📌 PYQ (2023).** Cefaclor is a cephalosporin of ______________ Generation.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Second generation</details>

**Q35. 📌 PYQ (2023).** Albendazole is a ___________ drug.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Anthelmintic</details>

**Q36.** What is bacteriocidal effect? *(📌 seen as a definition-style PYQ item)*
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The ability of a drug to directly kill bacteria (as opposed to merely inhibiting their growth, which is bacteriostatic).</details>

**Q37.** Name the drug of choice for radical cure of *P. vivax* malaria.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Primaquine</details>

**Q38.** Which enzyme does Metronidazole's activity depend on the microorganism being able to reduce it under — aerobic or anaerobic conditions?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Anaerobic conditions</details>

**Q39.** Name the class of antibiotics that must be avoided in children below 8 years due to teeth/bone effects.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Tetracyclines</details>

**Q40.** Name the class of antibiotics that must be avoided in children and pregnancy due to arthropathy/cartilage damage risk.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Fluoroquinolones</details>

**Q41.** Which anti-TB drug commonly causes optic neuritis?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ethambutol</details>

**Q42.** Which vitamin is co-administered with Isoniazid to prevent peripheral neuropathy?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6)</details>

**Q43.** Name the two major dose-limiting toxicities of Aminoglycosides.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ototoxicity and Nephrotoxicity</details>

**Q44.** Amphotericin B belongs to which class of anti-fungal agents?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Polyenes</details>

**Q45.** Name the neuraminidase inhibitor used for influenza.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Oseltamivir</details>

**Q46.** What must be checked before administering Primaquine?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>G6PD (glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase) status</details>

**Q47.** Name a luminal amoebicide used after Metronidazole to eradicate intestinal cysts.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Diloxanide furoate</details>

**Q48.** Which anti-neoplastic drug class includes Vincristine and Vinblastine?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Plant (vinca) alkaloids / mitotic inhibitors</details>

**Q49.** Name the drug of choice for lymphatic filariasis.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Diethylcarbamazine (DEC)</details>

**Q50.** Which class of drugs inhibits bacterial DNA gyrase?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Fluoroquinolones</details>

**Q51.** Co-trimoxazole is a combination of which two drugs?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Sulfamethoxazole + Trimethoprim</details>

**Q52.** What toxic reaction occurs if alcohol is consumed with Metronidazole?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Disulfiram-like reaction (flushing, nausea, palpitations)</details>

**Q53.** Which anti-fungal drug class inhibits ergosterol synthesis via CYP-dependent 14-α-demethylase?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Azoles</details>

**Q54.** Name the first-line anti-TB drug that causes orange-red discolouration of body secretions.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Rifampicin</details>

**Q55.** Which anti-malarial drug class acts fastest, via free-radical generation from an endoperoxide bridge?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Artemisinin derivatives</details>

**Q56.** Name the enzyme inhibited by Penicillins' target (penicillin-binding proteins) that results in bacterial cell lysis.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Transpeptidase (cross-linking enzyme in peptidoglycan/cell wall synthesis)</details>

**Q57.** What is the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC)?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The lowest concentration of a drug that inhibits visible growth of a microorganism.</details>
