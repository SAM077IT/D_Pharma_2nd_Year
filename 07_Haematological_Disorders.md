# Chapter 7 — Haematological Disorders
**Iron-deficiency anaemia and Megaloblastic anaemia | 4 hours**

## A. Anaemia
A condition in which haemoglobin concentration is below the normal range for the relevant population.

## Iron-Deficiency Anaemia

### Causes
- Chronic blood loss
- Poor dietary intake
- Increased requirement
- Malabsorption

### Features
Fatigue, pallor, weakness, dyspnoea and sometimes pica.

### Treatment
Identify and correct the cause + iron replacement.

Oral ferrous salts are commonly used.

### Counselling
- Take as directed
- GI upset/constipation may occur
- Dark stools can occur
- Vitamin C-containing food may improve iron absorption
- Tea/coffee around the dose may reduce absorption

## Megaloblastic Anaemia
Usually due to vitamin B12 or folate deficiency.

### Features
Fatigue, pallor, weakness. B12 deficiency may cause neurological manifestations.

### Treatment
- Folic acid for folate deficiency
- Vitamin B12 replacement for B12 deficiency
- Always distinguish B12 deficiency before treating isolated macrocytosis with folate alone because neurological injury may progress.
