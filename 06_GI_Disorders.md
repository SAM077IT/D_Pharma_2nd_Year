# Chapter 6 — Gastrointestinal Disorders
**GERD, Peptic Ulcer Disease, Alcoholic Liver Disease, IBD | 8 hours**

## A. GERD
Backflow of gastric contents into the oesophagus causing troublesome symptoms and/or complications.

### Symptoms
Heartburn, regurgitation, chest discomfort and sometimes chronic cough.

### Lifestyle
Weight management when appropriate, avoid personal trigger foods, avoid lying down soon after meals, stop smoking.

### Drugs
- Antacids for rapid short-term relief
- H2-receptor antagonists
- Proton-pump inhibitors (PPIs), such as omeprazole

## B. Peptic Ulcer Disease
An ulcer in gastric or duodenal mucosa, commonly associated with **H. pylori** infection or NSAID use.

### Symptoms
Epigastric pain, dyspepsia, nausea; complications include bleeding and perforation.

### Management
- Remove causative NSAID when possible
- Acid suppression
- Test and treat H. pylori when indicated

### H. pylori principle
Eradication requires a combination regimen containing acid suppression plus appropriate antibiotics, guided by local resistance patterns and current guidelines.

## C. Alcoholic Liver Disease
Liver injury related to harmful alcohol consumption.

### Management
- Complete alcohol cessation
- Nutritional support
- Manage complications
- Treat alcohol-use disorder where appropriate

## D. Inflammatory Bowel Disease
Two major forms:
- **Crohn's disease:** can affect any part of GI tract; transmural inflammation.
- **Ulcerative colitis:** primarily affects colon/rectum; continuous mucosal inflammation.

### Symptoms
Diarrhoea, abdominal pain, blood in stool, weight loss and fatigue.

### Treatment principles
Aminosalicylates in selected cases, corticosteroids for induction of remission, immunomodulators/biologics for selected patients, nutrition and surgery when indicated.
